# Recomputing the representation cells when the readout reassessment lands

Every representation-level verdict in the [capability map](D1_CAPABILITY_MAP_PLAN.md) is provisional until the [readout-class sweep](D1_READOUT_CLASS_SWEEP.md) and the [depth sweep](D1_READOUT_DEPTH_SWEEP.md) complete, after which the representation analyses at every gate are revisited and, where necessary, recomputed. This document owns that recomputation: the driver that refits a published cell at a selected readout class and depth, the inventory of what each gate retained for it, and the contract each gate's report is checked against. It owns no measurement. Every quantity, interval and verdict belongs to the gate record it is read from, and each gate record stays authoritative for its own numbers.

**Nothing has been recomputed.** The reassessment's results do not exist yet, so no selection exists to recompute at, and the driver refuses to run without one. What is built and verified is the machinery, the refusals and the retention accounting, so that the recomputation is a dispatch rather than a re-derivation when the selection arrives.

## The two selection kinds, and why they decide everything

A reassessment can return two different kinds of answer, and they have different retention requirements. Conflating them is the mistake this pipeline is built to prevent.

A **readout-class** selection is a different function of the four pooled summaries at the two blocks the admitted extraction hooked — a wider projection, the uncompressed features, a nonlinear head, or one depth-and-pooling block alone. Every gate that retained its full-width block outputs can serve it, with no forward pass anywhere.

An **extraction-depth** selection names a block other than those two, or a position-resolved summary. No retained array holds one. A gate can serve it only if a depth-resolved extraction exists **on that gate's own cohort**, and the depth campaign re-extracts the Readout cohort's 17 panels rather than any other cohort. For a gate fitted elsewhere, a depth selection is a fresh measurement campaign and not a refit, whatever that gate retained.

## The targeted three-depth extraction, planned and undispatched

The two depth-blocked gates matter more than an inconvenience, because the depth sweep resolves depth on its own cohort and finds information there: three residue-level protein arms resolve above zero at depths the admitted extraction never hooked, at the same block at all three split seeds, while the literal-text falsification control holds at every depth with 0 of 336 cells resolved above zero and 314 below. Until a depth selection can be applied to the stability and residue-interaction cohorts, a *not detected* reading on their representation halves cannot be distinguished from a measurement-limited one. Audit L53 carries that as an open limitation.

What removes it is not a full depth sweep of those cohorts but a targeted set: each arm's **selected** depth plus the two admitted depths retained alongside, so the new cells are comparable with the published ones on the same arrays. An arm whose selected depth already coincides with an admitted one hooks two rather than three. `src/transfer/targeted_depth.py` holds the plan at content digest `b85de9a89f08448fd69a1b616676acbd9ff60e7acfdd0d651da2dcfcc78cd01b` and `scripts/transfer/build_targeted_depth_campaign.py` writes it.

**The cost is measured, not estimated.** Capturing a third block is read from the same forward pass, so the GPU cost of a three-depth extraction is the wall clock the completed two-depth extraction of that cohort already recorded, arm by arm, from its own manifests. Only the retained storage grows, and it grows exactly in proportion to the depths hooked.

| Cohort | Arms | GPU-hours, measured | Retained state, 2 depths | Retained state, 3 depths | Longest arm |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 101 MegaScale groups, 25,957 states per arm | 33 | 13.891 | 29.75 GiB | 44.63 GiB | galactica-30b, 1.559 h |
| 64 cycle groups, 12,977 states per arm | 33 | 4.596 | 14.88 GiB | 22.31 GiB | galactica-30b, 0.792 h |
| Both | 66 cells | **18.487** | 44.63 GiB | **66.95 GiB** | — |

The storage model is validated against what the completed pass actually wrote: over the 33 arms of the stability cohort it gives 29.75 GiB at two depths against a measured 29.79 GiB, and the worst single arm differs by 0.002 GiB.

**The lanes are queueable.** Both extractors now accept a repeatable `--extra-block-index`, through one shared rule — `readout_extraction.hooked_block_indices` — which replaced the `[(len(blocks) - 1) // 2, len(blocks) - 1]` expression each of them carried. Four properties hold and are tested against both real extractors rather than a stand-in. With the option absent the hooked set is bit-identically what it was, for every block count in the panel and across the whole plausible range, which is what 33 arms of completed extraction and every published cell rest on. The admitted pair stays at feature positions 0 to 3 and extras are appended in ascending order after it, so an archive with extra depths still presents the admitted four blocks where a reader of the admitted layout expects them. The hooked set is recorded in the receipt twice over, as `block_indices` and as a `feature_blocks` name per block and pooling rule, so a later reader can tell which blocks an archive holds rather than inferring it from a count. And an index outside the stack is refused rather than clamped, because clamping would hook a block the caller did not ask for and record it as the one it did. The projected archive is untouched: the projection is still formed over the admitted four blocks alone under seeds 20260923–20260926, so a published cell reads a byte-identical array whether or not extra depths were hooked, and extras are retained at full width only — which is what a depth-resolved refit reads anyway, and inventing projection seeds for them would declare a contract nothing has declared.

The builder still reads each extractor's source before marking a campaign queueable, because that marking has to follow the code rather than the date it was written. **The one remaining blocking input is the per-arm depth selection** — or the depth-agnostic variant below, which needs none.

### A state-count estimate understates by 1.42, measured

This is the calibration any later estimate on these cohorts should carry, and it rests on one cohort. Scaling the stability cohort's measured total by the ratio of sequences scored predicts **3.530 GPU-hours** for the remote-homology cohort against the **5.028 measured** — the state-count ratio understates by a factor of **1.42**, because a longer target costs more per forward pass than a shorter one and a count of states does not see length. Carried through, Domainome's nominal **58.865** is nearer **83.9 GPU-hours** and a four-cohort sweep nearer **107.4**. The factor is reported beside the plain scaling rather than folded into it: it comes from a single cohort, and a second measured cohort would either confirm it or move it. Any future estimate built on state counts alone should carry both the factor and that caveat.

### The waves, reconsidered on the completed panel

The authorisation for the three waves was given on an interim reading, and the completed panel is a different argument for the same conclusion, so it is worth stating from the panel rather than inherited.

**The case is stronger than the interim reading made it, and for a different reason.** The interim reading rested on magnitude — a large increment at a block the admitted extraction never hooked — and that reading is now withdrawn: the best EC-panel block *straddles* the admitted pair and the anchor ceiling barely moves. What the completed panel establishes instead is breadth, and breadth is exactly what a two-depth retention costs: **7 arm-and-panel cells resolve at a single block at every seed having resolved nothing at the admitted pair at any seed, and six of those blocks are not members of the admitted pair**. That is a direct measurement that hooking two blocks misses arms — not a conjecture about what deeper blocks might hold. Four gates are still bound to two depths on their own cohorts, and for each of them a *not detected* representation reading is presently indistinguishable from a breadth-limited one. An every-block pass is the only thing that separates those two readings, and it is the cheaper half of the pipeline: 23.515 GPU-hours and 842.19 GiB for the three cohorts, against the 40 of 80 cells at the published selection that can otherwise never be recomputed at all.

**One qualification the completed panel adds.** Because the ceiling does not move, a wave will not raise any gate's increment; it can only change which arms carry one. So the waves buy a breadth correction at three gates, not a stronger result, and a reader expecting the latter should be told the former. The measured falsification control is what makes even that worth having: 0 of about 1,600 depth cells on the literal-text stratum resolve above zero, so a breadth gain that appears only on protein interfaces is not an artefact of looking at more blocks.

**The conclusion is unchanged: the three waves are justified, and Domainome remains deferred**, now for a second reason as well as its cost. Its cost is four times the other three together; the second reason is that a breadth correction is a correction to *which arms resolve*, and if its likelihood cell does not resolve there is nothing whose breadth anyone would read. Correcting the breadth of something nobody reads corrects nothing.

### Scope: three cohorts now, Domainome deferred

The sweep runs on the three cheap cohorts — folding stability at 13.891, residue interactions at 4.596 and remote homology at a measured 5.028 GPU-hours, **23.5 GPU-hours and about 842 GiB together** — and holds the Domainome one.

The reason is proportion, not parsimony. Domainome alone is 2,034.65 GiB and, on the calibrated figure, about 84 GPU-hours: more than four times the other three combined. And its confirmation claim does not yet exist even at the likelihood level — 9 of 33 arms extracted when last probed, and the sibling remote-homology gate's own likelihood half came back unresolved on both strata. If Domainome's likelihood cell also fails to resolve, a full-depth representation pass there would buy the recomputation of cells nobody would read. Deferring costs nothing in total work, because the GPU cost is per-pass whenever the pass runs; what it costs is time already being spent on that cohort's likelihood extraction. It is revisited when that lands.

One depth across both cohorts costs 22.32 GiB, and the 33 arms carry 871 blocks between them. The footprint is concentrated: `galactica-30b` at 48 blocks and width 7,168 takes 99.81 GiB and `qwen2.5-32b` at 64 blocks and width 5,120 takes 95.05 GiB, so those two arms are 27% of the full sweep.

**The allocation has 37,190.7 GiB free of 55,578.0 GiB, at 34% used**, measured on the store the archives would be written to, where `results/` already holds 1.2 TiB. A 720.18 GiB full sweep is **1.9% of the free space**.

**So a depth-agnostic pass is affordable and a subset is not worth its saving.** Every subset reintroduces exactly the conditionality the pass exists to remove: a depth the reassessment selects that falls on an unhooked block leaves the gate where L53 found it, and the depth sweep has already shown once that the admitted depths were the wrong place to look — +0.053010 [+0.029159, +0.077924] at block 34 of 36 against a near-null at the admitted pair. Halving the storage to buy back a fraction of that risk is the wrong trade at 1.9% utilisation. The recommendation is the full sweep, which also removes the per-arm selection from the critical path: it is currently the single blocking input for the whole recomputation, and a depth-agnostic pass does not need it.

Two bounds on that recommendation. The storage figure is for the full-width archives, and the depth sweep's own convention of one array per depth is what keeps a later fit able to stream one depth at a time rather than holding a stack; a fit over a 720 GiB corpus of states is a scheduling problem of its own, separate from the extraction. And 720.18 GiB is a measured arithmetic consequence of the retained archive shapes, validated at two depths against what the completed pass actually wrote, but it is not itself a measurement of a run that has happened.

`build_targeted_depth_campaign.py manifests --every-block` writes that variant and takes no selection; `--selection` writes the three-depth one. Neither dispatches.

**The archive layout is load-bearing, not incidental.** Beyond the admitted pair each hooked block is written as its own array, `features_d<index>`, holding that block's mean and last summaries. `np.load` on an npz reads a named array whole, so a fit over a 720 GiB corpus of states can stream one depth at a time only if each depth is its own array; a single `features` array per background would make the corpus a scheduling crisis instead. The default — no extra depths — writes exactly the one array it always wrote, byte for byte, because 33 arms of completed extraction and every published cell read that key. Anyone writing a fit over these archives should read one `features_d<index>` at a time and take the hooked order from the receipt's `block_indices` and `feature_blocks`.

### The dispatch decision, and what it is waiting on

The full every-block sweep is the decided variant. Its campaigns are written and frozen-ready as `scripts/transfer/campaign_depthall_gate_*.tsv`, one lane per cohort for `gpt2-large`, and `scripts/transfer/campaign_depthall_wave_*.tsv`, 32 lanes per cohort for the rest, so the gate arm is never extracted twice. The manifest reaches a pod only inside a code snapshot, so a dispatch is one `--freeze-only` plus one queue launch.

**One gate stood before the wave, and it is the property no test in this repository can otherwise reach.** `extract_batch`'s hooking loop needs a loaded checkpoint, so the order in which it registers hooks and concatenates their captures is preserved by construction rather than by measurement — and if that order moved, every archive in the sweep would be silently wrong while looking well formed. The gate is one arm extracted with `--every-block`, whose feature positions 0 to 3 must come out byte-identical to that arm's already-retained archive: a real end-to-end check on a loaded checkpoint against a known-good artefact.

**It passed.** `gpt2-large` on the stability cohort, every one of its 36 blocks hooked, 101 of 101 backgrounds and 25,957 sequences, cell `exited-ok` in **872.0 s against the 829.2 s** the same arm's two-depth extraction recorded — a 5% overhead for eighteen times the blocks, which is the pooling and the writing rather than the inference, and is the invariance claim measured instead of argued. Each archive holds 36 arrays of shape (rows, 2, 1280). Compared background by background against the retained two-depth archives, the admitted four block summaries are **byte-identical on all 101, at a maximum absolute difference of 0.0**. Every repeat check in the run read `repeat_delta_M_nats=0.0` with a relative L2 of about 4.0e-07 to 4.3e-07.

`scripts/transfer/verify_depth_archive_identity.py` is that comparison as a reusable stage, and it is itself tested on a faithful archive and on the failure that matters — the two admitted blocks swapped, so every number is present and every one is in the wrong place, which it must fail and does.

**All three cohorts are gated and all three pass.** Each was gated on its own cohort rather than on a sibling's result, because the hooking rule is shared but the cohort, the rendering and the archive layout are not.

### The waves, dispatched

Two of the three waves are in flight and the third is queued behind a card rather than behind a decision. One `--freeze-only` pinned at `17c9038` produced `RUN_ID=20260924211249_dfb821c212da`; the folding-stability and residue-interactions waves then went out as two independent campaign queues, one per four-card pod, and remote homology follows on the first pod to free.

They run one wave per pod because **every wave manifest claims cards 0 through 3**. Three queues on one pod would have refused 64 of 96 cells as `refused-busy-gpu` — a loud failure rather than a silent one, but 64 cells of wasted dispatch either way. At launch each campaign reported `slot 1 of 8`, `running=4`, `pending=28`, `skipped-complete=0`, `refused-busy-gpu=0`, `FAILURES 0`, `NO-RECORD 0`, and all eight cards were computing at 30 % to 94 % utilisation holding 5,361 to 128,253 MiB of the 143,771 MiB each card reports, one cell per card and none co-tenanted. The label collision catalogued as L54 is the defect this launch was watched for; it did not occur, because every label now carries its cohort and gate tag.

A tally is not an archive. The completion check is the tally reconciled against the archives on disk, cell by cell, rather than the tally alone.

**My own batch refused 24 cells of the folding-stability wave, and the guard is what caught it.** The recomputation batch and that wave were put on the same pod on the assumption that the batch was CPU-only. It was not. Each cell's device defaults to the device its *published* cell was fitted on — deliberately, because a ridge solve in another reduction order would confound a class change with a device change — and these cells were published on `cuda:0`, so six shards took **6,850 to 7,684 MiB** on the four cards. From slot 3 onward every folding-stability cell found its card occupied: **8 of 32 completed and 24 were `refused-busy-gpu`**, and the wave ran to its last slot refusing.

Nothing was lost and nothing was corrupted: a refused cell is never launched, so no archive was written, no tally was falsified and no card was co-tenanted. The idle-card gate did exactly what it exists for, and it is the reason this cost a re-dispatch rather than 24 silently degraded extractions. The assumption was mine and unverified; the fix is not to force the batch onto the host, which would reintroduce the confound the device default prevents, but to keep a recomputation batch and an extraction wave off the same allocation. **A recomputation batch is a GPU job whenever the cells it replays were published on a GPU.** The 24 refused arms re-dispatch as their own manifest with the complementary 8 excluded, so nothing is extracted twice.

**The prefix hazard, searched for and almost absent.** An arm name that is a prefix or substring of another can be silently attributed to the wrong arm by any match that is not anchored, and unlike a missing key it returns a plausible answer. The 33-arm campaign roster contains **6 such pairs over 3 arms**: `gpt2` is a prefix of `gpt2-large`, `gpt2-medium` and `gpt2-xl` and a substring of `protgpt2`; `prollama` is a prefix of `prollama-stage-1`; `qwen2.5-0.5b` is a prefix of `qwen2.5-0.5b-instruct`.

Searched across `src/transfer/` and `scripts/transfer/`, including the campaign layer, **one reachable case existed and it was in a runtime probe of mine**, which selected an arm's extraction manifest by testing whether the arm name appeared in the file name. It now resolves the manifest by the arm its own identity block declares, which is what the shipped adapter already did. Its one published result is unaffected and that was checked rather than assumed: the manifest list it actually saw held `progen3-3b` and `qwen2.5-0.5b` with no colliding pair, so the substring test had a unique hit.

Everything else matches exactly, and the campaign layer — where it would matter most, because a label is how the queue keys a resume — is clean by construction: labels are compared with string equality, the card index is matched as a whole `awk` field rather than a pattern, and the skip test is an existence check on a joined path with no glob. The two `startswith` calls on an arm name anywhere in the trees are deliberate family tests, `galactica-` and `progen3`, not identity resolution. That an unanchored match is absent from shipped code is the useful part of this result; that it appeared in a script written quickly, outside the tested surface, is where to expect it next.

### The defect the gate run caught, and where it actually lives

**The campaign queue derives a cell's output directory from the run id and the lane label alone**, and both cohorts' gate manifests labelled their cell `depth3-gpt2-large`. The second cell found the first's manifest at that path and was reported **skipped-complete** — writing nothing while presenting a clean status file. On the 32-arm wave that would have produced no archives at all for a whole cohort, under a tally showing every cell accounted for: a silent success, which is the failure mode this repository's principles put above all others. No reasoning about the hooking rule would have found it, because it was not in the hooking rule.

Every lane label now carries its cohort's own tag — `depth3-fs-`, `depth3-ri-`, `depth3-rh-`, `depth3-ec-` — the tags are asserted distinct by test, and the manifests were regenerated and re-frozen. The stability gate's verdict is unaffected: it ran first, under the old label, and its archives are the ones verified.

The hazard is catalogued as audit **L54**, scoped method and open, so it does not survive only as a paragraph here: the next person to meet it will meet it behind a clean tally, as this did.

**Whether the same collision is reachable elsewhere: it was reachable only from here, and the queue already enforces what it can.** `h200_campaign_queue.sh` refuses a manifest that names one label twice, with exactly the right diagnosis — *two cells would share one results directory; a resume key has no condition axis* — and exits 2. That check is central and correct, and it cannot see this collision, because the two cells were in **two manifests parsed by two launches**. Of the other campaign builders, `build_class_sweep_campaign.py` puts the arm, the panel and the seed in every label; `build_depth_campaign.py` spans one cohort, so it has no cross-cohort axis to collide on; and `build_nested_gate_campaign.py` takes a required `--gate` from a closed set and prefixes every label with it, which is why the remote-homology and Domainome waves sit in `rh-<arm>` and `ec-<arm>` directories. This builder was the only one that spanned more than one cohort without putting the cohort in the label, and the defect was its own.

**The residual gap is at the queue, and it is not another uniqueness assertion.** The skip is keyed on an expected artefact *existing* at the derived path, not on that artefact's recorded identity matching the cell about to run, so any two launches whose labels coincide — one builder or two, one session or months apart — silently resume each other's output. The smallest complete fix is for the skip to require that the artefact's own `identity` block names the arm and the plan digest of the cell it is about to skip; every extractor already writes one, so the check has its material. That change is **not made here**: `h200_campaign_queue.sh` is shared infrastructure whose success and failure paths are recorded as exercised in a real pod, three campaigns are running through it as this is written, and tightening skip semantics mid-flight could turn another agent's legitimate resume into a refusal. It belongs to whoever owns that layer, with its own validation pass, and is recorded here so the judgement is stated rather than implied by its absence.

So the sweep's gate is resolved and the wave is held only by the ordering below.

### The three adapter-blocked gates: two adapters, one dispatch

The 30 adapter-blocked cells needed two loaders and one dispatch, not three loaders. `src/transfer/recomputation_adapters.py` now holds both loaders. It sits beside the driver because these two gates are fitted by production *scripts* rather than by library modules, and loading a script by file location is a dependency the driver should not acquire in order to plan a cell it is not running. Each loader calls that stage's own profile-store binding, anchor-support refusal, packed-token budget, block builders and evaluator unchanged, so a recomputation reads the same arrays and fits the same designs as the published cell; what the adapter contributes is the representation block at the selected class, the identity refusals and the comparison.

Everything below was established by reading the artefacts and the stages' own command lines in a pod. Three things contradicted what the retention inventory assumed, and all three are corrected rather than accommodated.

**The declared metric names for crossed controls addressed no summary that gate has ever written.** The inventory named `representation_increment_over_C`, `representation_increment_over_C_P`, `representation_increment_over_C_L_P` and `representation_rank_mse_reduction_over_C_L_P`. The names are corrected and a test now derives the admissible set from the declared control sets and labels, so a name that no evaluator can write fails in the repository rather than in a campaign.

**The evolution-and-adaptation entry named a metric that exists nowhere.** That gate's record carries no flat summaries map at all: a stratum estimate is addressed as `readout_anchor[arm]['contrasts']['increment_R_C_P'][split seed][stratum]`, and `estimators` holds prose rather than numbers. Its declared metric is now the contrast its stage re-aggregates, and the declaration says that this gate is compared report against report through that stage rather than metric by metric through the driver's comparison.

So the adapter takes those two from that one stage whichever gate it is fitting; asking the local-context module for them finds nothing. ### One loader serves three gates, and two of them need no extraction at all

Both confirmation gates already declare the class axis recomputable, and a class refit at the already-hooked depths runs **no forward pass**, so their 99 cells each wait on code rather than on GPU time. They and the folding-and-stability gate all declare their designs in `src/transfer/stability_gate.py` and retain the same two archive kinds, so one stability-family loader serves all three and writing three would be writing one three times.

**The two archive kinds are the third thing the inventory understated, and the pattern is now the point.** Measured in-pod, each background writes two files: `full_<arm>_<background>.npz` holding a single `features` array of (rows, 4, hidden width) float32, and `<arm>_<background>.npz` holding `projected` at (rows, 4, 256) **beside the `likelihood` column and the pooled, scored and packed token counts, the token ids and offsets, the variant states and positions, the hidden width and two repeat controls**. A refit at a new class needs the first for the representation and the second for the likelihood, so a loader reading one kind is silently wrong — and the inventory had declared one directory per gate. The two kinds sit in one directory for the confirmation gates and in two separate runs for the stability gate, so the layout is a per-gate fact and not a family one. Volumes, measured: external confirmation 28,248 archives of both kinds over 33 arm directories at 139.37 GiB for the whole run directory, of which 126.09 GiB is the retained two-depth states; remote homology 20,130 archives at 8.51 GiB.

**The rule this pipeline produced.** *A retention declaration written from the writer's side records what a gate wrote, and a recomputation needs what it reads.* Write the declaration from the reader's side: name the file, its arrays, their shapes, which array carries which quantity, and the key a later run will look the record up by.

Four instances follow from it, and each cost a run or nearly did.

| What was declared | What a recomputation needed |
| --- | --- |
| A selection recorded per arm, as the cell that used it saw it | A selection looked up per **(arm, panel)**, because one arm selects different blocks on two panels |
| Two gates blocked at the depth axis, seen from each gate's own cohort | **Four** blocked, and the anchor cohort's depth archives already existing so 30 cells waited on nothing |
| One directory per gate, because the writer knew which file they meant | **Two archive kinds** per background, the representation in one and the likelihood in the other |
| Metric names as the record's prose describes them | The names the evaluator **writes**, `increment_R_C` and not `representation_increment_over_C` |
| A fit record binding four input digests | **Where those inputs are**, and which of the four is a file at all |

Every location in that declaration is a claim about a directory. A loader needs a file.

The fifth instance came from a published fit record rather than the inventory, and it sharpens the rule. `results/gate_stability_20260924/fits/fit_<arm>.json` binds `cohort_sha256`, `controls_sha256`, `profile_sha256` and `endpoint_sha256`, and records **no locations**: the record answers *is this the right artifact?* and not *where is it?*, which is the question a later run asks first. A digest without a location is a checksum, not a reference. Three of the four were located by hashing candidate files — the cohort, `controls_qualification.json` and `profile_features.npz`, all in the gate's own directory — and the fourth matched nothing among **1,668 JSON and 4,563 NPZ files** under `results/`, `data/` and `logs/`.

**It is not a missing artifact, and the reason is the sharper half of the finding.** `endpoint_sha256` is a **content** digest, not a file digest: the fit stage copies it from `cohort['endpoint_sha256']`, and it reproduces exactly from the cohort's own **25,856** variant records under the declared rule — background, sequence, position and ddG at ten significant figures, sorted by background then sequence. So the fit record *is* fully reproducible from what it binds. But four identically-shaped `*_sha256` fields mix file digests and content digests with nothing to distinguish them, so searching for a file with a content digest finds nothing and looks exactly like a provenance gap. The reader's-side rule therefore extends: a recorded digest should say **what it is a digest of** — a path, or the content and the rule that produced it — because a reader who guesses wrong cannot tell a missing artifact from a mis-framed search.

### The admitted tolerance does not transfer to the crossed-control family

The pipeline-identity replay of the `qwen2.5-0.5b` crossed-control cell **refused**, and the refusal is the result rather than an obstacle to it.

Every design containing the representation block departs from the published held-out predictions by **1.58e-6 to 2.94e-6** — `C+R` at 2.93175e-06, `C_P+R` at 2.43538e-06, `C_L+R` at 1.69161e-06, `C_L_P+R` at 1.58412e-06, `C_L_P_T+R` at 1.58841e-06, and each of their `+M` counterparts — while **every design without it agrees inside 1e-8**. The split is exactly the R boundary, which is also a width boundary: the designs that agree are 444 to 1,123 columns, those that do not are 1,468 to 2,147.

Three measurements locate the cause. The representation block itself is **byte-identical** to the published one on this arm and support, so the features are not the difference. Two independent runs of the identical replay on one host produce **bit-identical deviations**, so the computation is deterministic and the departure is to the published fit's environment rather than run-to-run noise. And the estimator solves the ridge through a symmetric eigendecomposition of the float64 covariance, so a design carrying 1,024 random-projection columns presents a dense near-degenerate small-eigenvalue spectrum whose eigenvector basis is numerically ill-determined; two reduction orders then give coefficients differing at about 1e-6, while the narrower, better-conditioned designs reproduce.

The 1e-8 tolerance is the one the admitted receipt declares, and it was set and validated for the **readout family's** own replay. It is below this family's cross-host reproducibility, and the published cells record neither a thread count nor a host, so the published reduction order cannot be reconstructed from the record. **The tolerance has not been loosened**, and a tolerance chosen because it lets a comparison pass is not a control. If one specific to this family turns out to be needed it is declared for the crossed-control family with the eigenbasis reason and its measurement beside it, never by relaxing the readout family's gate.

**What the departure is measured against is the estimand, not the arithmetic path.** A near-degenerate spectrum has many nearly equivalent bases, so the prediction is that the solution is stable where the basis is not. The driver was restructured to answer it: the pipeline gate now *decides* instead of raising, every cell is carried through to its increments, its intervals and a comparison of **every** summary the two reports share — point, both interval endpoints, resampling unit, unit count and whether the interval excludes zero — and the record is written before the refusal is raised. A cell that fails the gate is marked `verdict: refused` and the runner exits non-zero, so nothing is reported as having passed a gate it failed; what changes is that the refusal arrives with the evidence rather than in place of it. Suppressing the comparison in order to raise earlier was the one thing that made the real question unanswerable from the artefact.

The sensitivity ordering explains itself and is worth stating, because it generalises past this cell. A Spearman increment is a function of the held-out predictions' **ranks**, and a 1e-6 perturbation of well-separated predictions changes no rank, so those increments are recovered bit-for-bit. A rank-MSE reduction is a function of the predictions' **values**, so it does move — by about 1e-9, three orders below the perturbation, because it is a difference of two mean squared errors in which the shared perturbation largely cancels. Bit-identity is therefore reported and is not the verdict: only 6 of 72 summaries are bit-identical, and the floor is last-bit float64 arithmetic reached even by `raw_P_spearman`, a quantity carrying no representation at all, whose published and recomputed values differ by one ulp. What a reader should weigh is the two maxima against the five decimal places these records report — the movement sits four orders of magnitude below the last reported digit.

So the gate was checking a quantity to which no published number of this cell is sensitive. That does not make the gate wrong to have fired: the departure is real, it is deterministic, and it is unattributable while the receipts carry neither thread count nor host. It makes the **refusal informative rather than blocking** — the cell is marked refused and the record states, in the same artefact, that every published quantity survived. A pipeline verified only at the prediction level would have reported this cell as a failure and said nothing about its increments; one verified only at the increment level would have missed the environment difference entirely.

**The forward fix this finding implies.** The published cells record neither the thread count nor the host, which is exactly why the publishing environment cannot be reproduced. A receipt should carry both from now on, alongside the resampler digest at function granularity that L55 names: between them they are what turns a departure like this one from an unattributable difference into a named difference in reduction order.

**A defect in this runner, found by running it rather than reading it.** The selection is keyed by arm **and** panel, because ProGen3-3B selects block 11 on the anchor panel and block 23 on the EC-conditioned one. The roster resolver looked up a bare arm, so it matched nothing and every roster was refused with "the selection declares no class and depth for arm …" — `plan` and `run` were both unusable, while the tests passed because they built requests directly rather than through the roster. It is fixed, keyed by the pair, and a cell whose panel the reassessment did not select for is refused with that arm's selected panels named. The failure was loud, which is the right direction; serving the arm's other panel would have been the substitution this driver exists to prevent.

## The driver

`src/transfer/recomputation.py` with `scripts/transfer/recompute_representation_cells.py`. Three subcommands, in the order they are used.

`plan` joins a cell roster with the reassessment's per-arm selection and reports which cells can run and which cannot, before any array is read. `replay` refits one cell at the **admitted** selection and is the pipeline-identity control; it is the only subcommand that runs before the reassessment lands. `run` is the recomputation itself.

A cell is a gate, an arm, a panel, a split seed, a selection and the path to the published cell report. The support is not an input: it is read from the published report, so a cell is fitted on the rows its own published increment was fitted on rather than on a separately declared support that could differ. The fitting procedure is the gate's own entry point, called unchanged — for the Readout family, `readout_analysis.evaluate_readouts` — so the folds, the targets, the equal cluster and assay and variant weights, the penalty grid, the tie rule, the label-permutation control and the resampling contract are the published cell's and not this driver's. What the driver contributes is the representation block at the selected class and depth, the identity refusal, and the comparison.

Each recomputed cell reports every published metric of its gate with the recomputed point and interval beside the published point and interval, the resampling unit and draw count both were formed under, the change in the point estimate, and whether the resolved sign changed. A metric whose resampling unit differs between the two reports is marked not comparable with the reason, rather than differenced.

### The refusal conditions

The driver refuses rather than proceeding when any of the following holds. Each is a case in which the recomputed number would not be a recomputation of the published one.

- **Support.** The ordered assay support differs from the published cell's. A different set is refused with the absent and added assays named; the same set in a different order is refused separately, because the fitted rows are concatenated in assay order.
- **Label budget and support counts.** The unit count or the variant count differs.
- **Split seed and folds.** The split seed differs, or any outer fold's held-unit membership differs, or any inner fold's membership differs. The seed alone does not certify the partition: a different unit set under the same seed gives a different partition, so membership is compared directly.
- **Resampling contract.** The resampling unit label, the draw count or the resampling seed differs.
- **Reduction order.** The process has no discoverable BLAS thread pool, so the admitted float32 reduction order cannot be pinned.
- **Depth without a depth-resolved extraction.** The selection names a block or a position-resolved summary the retained states do not hold, or omits the coordinate count or projection seed its axis requires.
- **Pipeline identity.** At the admitted selection, the refitted held-out predictions depart from the published ones by more than the tolerance the admitted admission receipt declares. That tolerance is read from `results/transfer/readout_20260923/final_admission.json` — `baseline_tolerance.atol`, 1e-8 — and a missing or non-positive value in that receipt is itself a refusal. A replay that invented its own tolerance would certify nothing.
- **No selection.** `run` and `plan` refuse without the reassessment's own declaration of class and depth per arm.
- **No adapter, or no cells.** A gate with no representation cell, a gate whose retention cannot serve the selection kind, a gate with no implemented adapter, or a cell whose published report is absent, is refused at plan time with what is missing named.
- **A retained archive that is not there.** An archive the extraction manifest lists and the filesystem does not hold is reported as a refusal naming the file, because that is a retention finding rather than a fault in the loader.

At a selection other than the admitted one the prediction departures are reported and **not** gated: at a new class or depth the held-out predictions are supposed to move, and gating them there would refuse the recomputation for doing its job. What must not move is the support, the folds, the seeds, the weighting and the label budget, and those are gated at every selection.

### Thread count and per-assay blocking

Two properties of the admitted compressed block are part of pipeline identity, and both are delegated to the modules that own them rather than restated: the BLAS thread count is pinned inside `readout_class_sweep.projected_block`, and the rows are blocked by assay through `readout_depth.admitted_design`. The driver adds a measurement rather than a second implementation: `product_blocking_departure` reports how far the whole-panel product sits from the per-assay one on the arrays at hand, with the largest absolute entry it is read against and the BLAS pools it was measured under, so the constraint appears as a number in the cell report.

Measured on one retained extraction this host holds — three assays and nine variants of `proteinglm-7b-clm` at hidden width 4,096 — the driver's per-assay product is bit-identical to an independently assembled per-assay product, the per-assay product is identical at 4 and at 48 BLAS threads at a maximum absolute difference of 0.0, the whole-panel product moves between those two thread counts by up to 5.722 × 10⁻⁶, and the whole-panel product departs from the per-assay one by up to 6.199 × 10⁻⁶ against a largest absolute entry of 18.236. Both departures sit at the same relative scale as the 7.629 × 10⁻⁶ against 723.245 the depth sweep measured on its 212-assay panel, which is what makes the pin and the blocking necessary rather than decorative.

Every identity digest renders each label as a Python value before hashing. `repr(np.float64(0.5))` is `0.5` under NumPy 1.26 and `np.float64(0.5)` under NumPy 2, and `json.dumps` refuses a NumPy integer outright, so a fold label or an assay identifier arriving as a NumPy scalar would either hash differently across interpreters or fail to serialise — the same defect that cost the global-context gate a run on its row-identity digest.

## The unified gate contract

`src/transfer/gate_contract.py`, content digest `bfe873ba3382c1728842dc08afa26d276ab5084ee5b49022040311595bbc2533`, with `scripts/transfer/check_gate_contract.py`. The contract is the six steps the capability map already declares — endpoint qualification, control qualification, native likelihood behaviour, representation readout, held-family and remote generalization, and causal validation where justified — written in a form a reader or a test can check a gate against. **No gate is refactored onto it.** Each gate keeps its own modules, scripts and record, and what the contract holds is a read-only transcription of the elements each record already carries.

Four rules carry the substance, and each exists because a gate record shows why. A control must carry its own contribution in one of three declared forms. A representation element may not be reported as settled while the reassessment is open. A likelihood element is not deferred, because a likelihood enters as one scalar column and a linear readout of it is close to the full function space over that feature. And a step not reached is named — *unresolved*, *not detected*, *not applicable* and *absent* are four states, never merged — with its reason.

**The three qualification forms are what the first pass of this check got wrong, and the correction is in the contract rather than in the gate.** The form every gate with a fitted readout uses is an **interval**: the control's paired contribution to the baseline it augments, with its resampling interval, so that a control indistinguishable from zero is visible rather than implied — the stability gate keeps a block whose own contribution is +0.00390 [−0.00068, +0.00864] kcal²/mol² and records that this makes *beyond controls* weaker than the phrase suggests. The generation-and-control gate cannot use that form and does not need it, because its controls are generators rather than feature blocks: what has to be established is that each emits the statistics it declares and nothing more, which is a property of every row rather than of an average over them. So **exactness** — a decidable property checked on all 14,467 pairs, as for the corpus fragment's 0 containment and 0 length failures — and **order_statistic** — a signed position on a declared axis, as for the order-4 Markov cohort's −0.0007 nats per residue against a length-matched corpus reference — are declared forms of their own, each carrying what its qualification establishes in place of an interval. Forcing those into interval form would be a weaker claim about a stronger fact, so the contract accepts them and the gate record now states for each control why its form is the tight one. An interval-form contribution that carries no interval and declares no other form is still a divergence.

What makes the check read-only and still meaningful is the transcription check: every quantity a declaration names must occur verbatim in the record it points at. All 78 declared quantities do.

**All fifteen retention entries are declared, and all fifteen conform.** The nine gates with records of their own, the three upstream studies whose retained arrays four of the gates read — the readout panel, the crossed-control study and the D2 ProLLaMA transplant — and the three unlaunched gates, which point at the capability map that states their admission conditions and declare every step absent with its reason, so that an unlaunched gate is a recorded state rather than an omission. The D2 transplant is the one entry whose causal step is reported rather than absent: a parameter transplant is an intervention on the checkpoint, and it localises which group of parameters carries a measured likelihood difference without identifying any computation inside the model.

#### Above the entries that follow: the failure existed and the system could not see it

Five failures in this work are one failure with five subjects, and it belongs above them rather than beside them: **I had made the failure unobservable.** In each case the work's actual state and the apparatus's report of it were allowed to differ and nothing in the apparatus could tell the two apart — a guard whose threshold sits outside the range of the statistic it guards, a bound below its support's noise floor, a watcher condition that cannot become true, a watcher condition that cannot be evaluated, and a launcher whose output goes to `/dev/null`. The first four are checks that cannot fail; the fifth is evidence discarded before a reader could reach it; and what they share is that a healthy run and a broken one produce byte-identical output.

Each instance keeps its measurement where it was made — the 0.21 separation and the 78 of 2,310 at risk, the 0.083 bound against a 0.125 standard error, the four watcher conditions and the five hours they cost, and the ten discarded runners under *Every remote write goes through one pattern* below. Two more of the family were measured elsewhere in the same days and stay there: a patch script whose failed assertion discarded the edits it had already applied while every surrounding command reported success, and a test run behind `tail` whose exit status was the pipe's rather than pytest's, both in the experiment log's record of the manuscript session. The audit's Appendix B rule 36 is where that pair's general form already lives. What generalises is neither any one instance nor a sixth entry, but the two questions that would have caught all of them: **can this condition be false on the data it will actually see, and can this run's failure reach a reader?**

**A condition that cannot fire is not a check.** The per-design absolute correlations and the raw model and profile correlations are *not* a control here: they sit 0.21 or more from zero, only **78 of 2,310** are at risk at all, and their zero crossings measure their distance from zero rather than their stability. Saying so is what makes the 254 credible — the same at-risk test that licenses the likelihood families disqualifies these.

**And its mirror: a threshold below its support's noise floor always fires, which makes it no more a check than one that never does.** A test of the D2 spectrum-matched control bounded the correlation between a synthesised tensor and its donor at a fixed 0.05 and read **0.083** on an 8×8 fixture, where the standard error of a correlation between two uncorrelated vectors of 64 elements is **1/√64 = 0.125** — so the bound sat inside its own noise and would have failed on any draw. The repair is a bound that scales with the support it is applied on, 4/√n, which is 0.125-scale on those fixtures and 0.00024 on the 4096-square tensors the control actually runs on. Both failures are one failure: a threshold uncalibrated to the support it is applied on, firing always or never according to which side of the noise floor it fell.

That principle is not confined to measurement guards. **Four** campaign watchers in this work failed the same way, each a condition that could not report rather than a fault in the work it watched:

1. a `pgrep -f` pattern that matched the watcher's own command line, so the process count was never zero;
2. a campaign basename composed from an infix that resolved to no status file;
3. an exact `shards_complete=6`, which could not fire once a seventh shard existed;
4. a marker count summed through `bc`, **which is not installed in the pod**, so the arithmetic produced nothing silently.

The fourth is the sharpest, because the first three were conditions that could not be *true* and this one could not be *evaluated*. Its cost is worth writing down at the scale it actually was: a panel complete at 16:31Z with its six completion markers on disk was not acted on for **five hours**. Nothing scientific was lost — the fits were on disk throughout — and the contrast is the evidence, because the watcher that counts with `grep` and `wc` and no arithmetic tool has fired correctly every time.

**The construction rules, fixed once rather than instance by instance**, and written into the watcher itself so they travel with it: key on the campaign's own artefacts and never on `pgrep`; make the condition **monotone or terminal-state based**, never exact equality over a set that can grow; compute it with the fewest external dependencies; and **before trusting it, run it against an already-complete campaign and confirm it fires at once.**

That last rule is a **positive control for the watcher**, and it is the only one of the four that would have caught all four failures — each of the others would have passed its own defect. It is what the cannot-fire principle implies and had not been stated as a procedure. Done here: the repaired watcher run against the completed remote-homology panel fired on its first check, `shards_complete=6 shards_expected=6`, exit 0. A watcher that has never been shown to fire is an assumption, not a check.

**A fifth rule, earned by the repair rather than by a campaign: the completion condition must be able to be false on the directory it is pointed at.** The repaired watcher compared `shards_complete` against `shards_expected` and both are zero on a gate whose shard lists are not named `*.arms`, so it reported a panel complete that had not begun — the cannot-fire failure in its mirror form, inside the fix for it. The shard-list glob is an argument now, at least one shard must exist before the comparison decides, and the watcher is validated in three directions rather than one: it fires at once on a synthetic complete panel, and does **not** fire on an empty directory or on a shard that has started and not finished. Stated plainly, because it is the sharpest of the five: the cannot-fire failure was inside the fix for cannot-fire, which makes this the positive-control rule applied to its own implementation.

## The stability-family loader is blocked on a design decision, not on code

Surveying the four gates' own fit stages before writing their loader produced two findings, and the second stops the depth-axis half of the work until someone decides what a depth selection means for this family.

**Residue interactions needs its own contrast, and that is now known rather than discovered at refusal time.** All four gates read the same array names from their archives — `projected`, `likelihood`, the token descriptors — so one archive reader serves them. What differs is the feature: `fit_stability_singles.py` forms the single-mutant difference `projected[state] − projected[0]`, and `fit_pairwise_epistasis.py` forms a double-mutant cycle contrast over four states. A single loader computing one of those would produce a well-shaped, plausible and wrong feature for the other gate, which is the failure mode a refusal cannot catch because nothing about the result looks malformed. So the adapter must call **each gate's own loader** and substitute only the representation source, exactly as the anchor-panel adapters call their stages' builders.

**The blocker: the stability family's representation width is asserted, and a depth selection does not preserve it.** Its published representation is a mutation difference over the **four** admitted pooled summaries at **two** depths — 1,024 coordinates — and `fit_stability_singles.load_arm` refuses anything else outright: *representation width is not four projected blocks*. The landed depth archives hold `features_d000` through `features_d047`, each of shape `(rows, 2, width)`: **mean and last only, no position-resolved summaries**, because these cohorts render no one-token-per-residue grid. The reassessment's selection asks for 256 coordinates per summary, so a depth selection yields **2 × 256 = 512**. The gate's own loader therefore refuses the selection — correctly, since 512 coordinates of one depth are not 1,024 coordinates of two.

`fit_pairwise_epistasis.py` carries **no** such width assertion. So the same substitution fails loudly on three gates and silently on the fourth, and the silent one is the dangerous one. A loader written past this would deliver three refusals and one half-width design presented as a recomputation.

What resolves it is a decision about the gates rather than a loader: either the selection declares, for this family, a coordinate count that preserves the 1,024-wide design — 512 per summary would — or the family's design is declared as changing width at a depth selection, which changes what its increment is comparable to and belongs in its own record. Neither is a choice a loader should make, and the width assertion is the reason the first option is even visible.

**What needs no decision** is the admitted-axis replay: there the representation is the published four-block projection, the width is 1,024 by construction, and the substitution is byte-identical. That is the verification this loader should be gated on, and it can be run before the question is settled.

**These cohorts render no position-resolved summaries at all, and that is a property of the cohorts rather than a retention gap.** The depth archives hold `features_d000` through `features_d047` and nothing else: two summaries per block, mean and last. There is no `mut` or `suffix` array because a position-resolved summary requires the wild type and each of its mutants to render to the same one-token-per-residue grid, and these cohorts do not. So **the position-resolved half of the depth sweep's findings has no counterpart on these four gates**, and anyone reading the sweep's boundary-lifted cells against them needs to know that before trying: those cells rest on summaries these archives could not contain.

**Fixing a single-source violation created a worse one, briefly.** The width condition existed inline in **two** stages, each with its own copy of the message, and not at all in the third. Adding the shared helper beside the two copies made **three** expressions of one condition — the act of fixing the violation made it worse until the copies were removed. Both are now converted, no inline copy remains in `scripts/` or `src/`, and the guard is in the path for all four gates rather than bypassed for any. The order that avoids this is to look for existing copies before adding the canonical one, not after.

Converting two production fit stages meant the 1e-13 replay had been produced by code that no longer existed, so it was re-run on the converted stages: **919 numbers at a maximum absolute difference of 0.0**, the `primary` block byte-identical, and `generated_utc` the only differing field in the whole record. The conversion is behaviour-preserving as measured, not as argued.

**The width condition is now shared and all three stages refuse.** `src/transfer/pairwise_epistasis.require_projected_width` states the condition once — the admitted design is `len(FEATURE_BLOCKS) * PROJECTION_DIM` coordinates, four pooled summaries at 256 each — and `fit_pairwise_epistasis.load_arm` calls it per archive, naming the arm and the file in the refusal. The stability stage already refused this; the pairwise one did not, so the same substitution failed loudly on three gates and silently on the fourth, and the silent direction is the one that yields a plausible wrong number. That is a defect independent of any decision about the depth axis, and it is closed. Its own 38-test suite still passes.

### An operational hazard: a shadowing module in the allocation's /tmp

A `dis.py` sits in the allocation's `/tmp`. Python puts a script's own directory first on the import path, so **any in-pod script run from `/tmp` shadows the standard library's `dis` module**, and the failure surfaces as `AttributeError: module 'dis' has no attribute 'COMPILER_FLAG_NAMES'` raised from inside `numpy`'s own import chain — a symptom that points at NumPy, at the runtime, or at nothing, and never at a file in the working directory.

The file is not ours and is not deleted. The rule that avoids it is the one that already saved the probes here: **an in-pod script runs from the snapshot directory**, which is where the code it imports lives anyway. Writing a probe to `/tmp` and running it there is the pattern to avoid; writing it under the run's own ignored log directory and running from the snapshot is the pattern that works.

### Every remote write goes through one pattern

Three times in this work a nested heredoc inside a quoted remote command destroyed the indentation of the Python it was writing, most recently on the local-context runner (`IndentationError: expected an indented block after 'for' statement on line 23`). On that launch the shards' output went to `/dev/null`, so **ten of twelve died leaving no trace** and the two that survived are the only reason the launch was known to have failed at all.

**And for a file, the right mechanism was there all along.** `~/hangzhou-compute/ssh_tunnel/h200_gpfs_push.sh <local file> <gpfs path>` pushes a file and **verifies its SHA256 on the far side**, which is what the base64 pattern was reconstructing by hand — and unlike the command line it has no argument-length limit. A 2.8 MB payload had already failed with `/usr/bin/timeout: argument list too long`, and a stdin attempt produced a silently **empty** file whose emptiness was visible only because the digest was checked. So: **a file goes through `h200_gpfs_push.sh`; base64 on the command line is for small inline payloads only; stdin is not forwarded to a pod at all; and either way the transferred copy is verified by digest before it is used.** The rest of this entry stands as the rule for the inline case.

The remedy already existed and its **scope** was the defect: it had been applied to probes, which is where the mangling had last bitten. So the rule is the scope and not the technique. **Every** write to the allocation goes through the pattern that has not failed once, whatever the file is for: write it locally, `base64 -w0` it, decode it in the pod, assert `ast.parse` on the decoded copy in the pod before anything runs, run it from the snapshot or from the run's own log directory rather than `/tmp`, and give every background shard its own error file. Reading back takes the same care, and it was tested immediately: pulling a consolidation artefact with `base64 -w0` and no trailing newline let the transport's own `H200_REMOTE_EXIT=0` marker land on the payload's last line, which `base64 -d` then refused. That failure was visible because the transferred file is verified against its in-pod SHA256 rather than assumed to have arrived.

### The admitted-axis replay of a stability-family cell reproduces at 1e-13

This is the recomputation for these four gates, and it needed no decision about the depth axis: at the admitted selection the representation is the published four-block projection, the width is 1,024 by construction, and the fit is the gate's own stage run again on the inputs the published record binds.

Located by digest rather than by path, because the record carries no locations: the cohort, `controls_qualification.json` and `profile_features.npz` in the gate's own directory, and the extraction at `results/external_baseline/20260924000148_fbfa7ddc1f5f/gpt2-large`, 101 archives and a manifest. `gpt2-large` refitted on CPU at eight threads.

Every identity field is identical — the arm, all four input digests, the three split seeds, the row, group and site counts, the qualified control set, the schema, the tokenisation stratum and the matched baseline. The numbers then reproduce to **1e-13**: **546 numbers of the `primary` block at a maximum absolute difference of 8.085e-14**, and **373 of the rest of the record at 3.553e-14**. The largest single departure is an interval endpoint at 0.05123990066284471 against 0.051239900662763856.

**This narrows an open question from earlier in the batch, and the class-axis consolidation turns it into a pattern.** The crossed-control family's admitted replay departed by 1.58e-6 to 2.94e-6 on every design containing the representation block, recorded as unattributable while the receipts carry neither thread count nor host. It ran on **this same host and interpreter**, so *host alone* was already excluded. The consolidation extends it: over **four design families** — crossed controls, local context, folding stability and external confirmation, on three cohorts with three control ladders and two fit stages — the two whose representation is a **wide random projection** depart at 1e-6 to 1e-5 — the local-context gate's full 99-cell panel reaches 4.49e-6 in a point and 1.24e-5 in an interval endpoint, against 2.86e-15 and 8.66e-15 on its own likelihood quantities in the same cells — and the two whose representation is a **mutation difference over a fixed projection** reproduce at **1e-16**. That is a consistent pattern rather than a single-cohort coincidence, and it supports the conditioning account: a design carrying a wide random projection presents a near-degenerate covariance whose eigenbasis is numerically ill-determined, while a narrow fixed one does not.

**It is a pattern and not a controlled result, and the test that would settle it is named here deliberately unrun.** Refitting one cell twice on the same cohort and the same control ladder — once with the narrow fixed projection, once with a wide random one — would isolate the projection as the cause, because everything else would be held. It is cheap and decisive. It is **not** being run: it governs how a numerical-reproducibility limitation is described, not any claim about models, and nothing in the manuscript rests on it. A later reader who needs the controlled result has the design here rather than deriving it again, and knows that what exists today is four families agreeing, not one variable isolated.

### Three sibling stages, three conventions, and none discoverable from the interface

The four gates are fitted by three stages that look interchangeable and are not. Two differences each cost a launch, both failed loudly, and neither is visible without trying.

**The extraction argument means different things.** `fit_pairwise_epistasis.py` does `load_arm(args.extraction / args.arm, ...)`, appending the arm itself; `fit_stability_singles.py` and `fit_nested_singles.py` take the arm's own directory. Passing the child to the first produces `extraction/<arm>/<arm>/manifest_<arm>.json` and 33 immediate refusals with nothing written.

**The BLAS thread pool is pinned in one stage and free in another, and both are defensible.** `fit_nested_singles.py` refuses anything but four threads — *BLAS thread count must be pinned to 4* — and rejected all 33 of external confirmation's arms at eight. `fit_stability_singles.py` takes `--threads 8` without comment. The pin exists because the thread count sets a float32 reduction order, which is what cost the class sweep its first C0 gate, and it is load-bearing for a stage forming a wide random projection in a way it is not for one forming a single-mutant difference. This work's own evidence says so: the stability replay at **eight** threads reproduced its published fit at **1e-13** over 919 numbers. So the honest statement is that **the pin is enforced where the reduction order is load-bearing and absent where it is not, and no stage documents which it is — a caller learns by refusal.** Either all three pin, or each states whether it does and why. Recording it is enough with four campaigns in flight.

**The 19 folding-stability cells that ran at eight threads are not being re-run, and this is the reasoning so it is not re-litigated.** They are a screen under the device protocol above: a cell is reported from the screen unless its verdict changes, and a verdict-changing cell is confirmed at its published setting. The thread count each cell ran at is recorded with it, because the protocol depends on that being written down rather than assumed — shards 0 to 6 of this gate ran at `OMP_NUM_THREADS=8`, external confirmation's at 4 after its stage refused 8, residue interactions' at 8. ### An expressiveness failure, not an accuracy one, and the method it forces

Folding stability's extraction holds **31 arms** under `20260924000148_fbfa7ddc1f5f` and **two** — `galactica-30b` and `qwen2.5-32b` — under `20260923232409_9ab5f150f2ac`, presumably because the two largest arms were extracted separately. A launcher deriving a per-arm path from one run id fails on exactly those two, which is how `galactica-30b` failed here, and the sharded-directory hypothesis was wrong: there is no `galactica-30b` directory in that run under any name.

**This is a different kind of problem from the five before it, and the difference decides the remedy.** A misattributed store, a glob that stopped at the top level, and a content digest mistaken for a file digest were all **accuracy** failures — a declaration that was wrong and could be corrected. A gate whose arms live across two extraction runs cannot be expressed by a path template carrying one run id at all: the format has no way to say it. That is an **expressiveness** failure, and filing it as a sixth instance of the same thing would hide the one instance that needs a different fix.

**The remedy is content resolution, and it is now a method rather than an expedient.** Where a layout cannot be declared, declare how to find it and record what was found: scan every run for `*/<arm>/manifest_<arm>.json`, keep only manifests whose identity declares this gate's own schema, and require exactly one candidate per arm. That gives **33 of 33 arms, one candidate each, no ambiguity**. The resolved map is written as an artifact carrying the scan rule beside it, so the next run neither re-scans nor inherits a template that cannot express the truth.

This is the **second** time content resolution was necessary rather than convenient — the first was locating the five pairwise inputs by digest, where four filenames matched and the fifth did not because it was a content digest. Two necessities make it a method.

**The asymmetry is the argument, and it is worth more than the three corrections.** A layout assumption about this repository's artifact trees has now been wrong **three times in one day** — a store, a search depth, and a sharding shape — while content resolution has been right **every time it was tried**. Declared paths are a convenience that must be checked; declared content is what identifies an artefact.

**And a count of entries is not a count of arms.** That run directory holds three empty `full-*` directories with zero archives, so it shows 34 entries against 31 manifests. An empty directory that looks like an arm is exactly what makes a count disagree with a manifest scan, and someone counting entries rather than manifests would have concluded the extraction covered 34 arms when it covers 31.

### Scope and device protocol for the four-gate consolidation

**Two axes, two scopes, for different reasons.** The class selection is `C4_full_random_feature` for every cell, and a gate's verdict is a **panel-level statement**, so the class-axis refit runs the **full 33-arm panel** for each gate: consolidating from a subset would answer a different question, and narrowing to the arms that resolved before would make a newly resolving arm undetectable — which is exactly the breadth the reassessment found the admitted measurement short on. The depth arm is the opposite case: no depth is selected outside the ten arm-and-panel pairs the selection names, so it runs on **those only**, and that is a restriction of scope rather than of effort.

**The device is a screen, and the screen is bounded by measurement.** Every cell's device default is the device its published cell was fitted on, deliberately, so that a ridge solve in another reduction order cannot be mistaken for a class change. Forcing the panel onto the host for throughput reintroduces exactly that confound — so it is done as a **screen** and not as a conclusion, and the bound is measured rather than assumed: a cross-environment design departure of 1.58e-6 to 2.94e-6 moved published points by at most **2.10e-9** and interval endpoints by **9.14e-9**, four orders below the last digit these records report, with **zero** resolved sign changes.

The protocol that follows from that, stated so a reader knows which cells were screened and which were confirmed:

1. The panel runs on the host for throughput, and **every cell records the device it was fitted on**.
2. A cell whose verdict does **not** change is reported from the screen, because the measured device effect is four orders below the reported precision.
3. A cell whose verdict **does** change is **re-run on its published device before the change is reported**. A verdict change is the one output where a 1e-9 movement can matter, because crossing a boundary is not a magnitude. That is a handful of cells and it costs almost nothing.

No conclusion in the consolidation therefore rests on a device difference, and the record says of each cell which of the two it is.

### The matched-capacity depth contrast, and the seed it uses

A depth selection cannot be a recomputation of this family's published cell, because the admitted design pools four summaries across **two** depths by construction: selecting one depth is not a narrower version of it but a differently constructed measurement, and forcing the width back to 1,024 would need a projection seed nothing declares. So the depth question is asked as a **matched contrast at equal capacity** instead: the selected depth's `mean` and `last` at 256 coordinates each against **an admitted depth's** `mean` and `last`, also 512, both drawn from the same archives and the same declared projection, with only the depth varying.

**The seed is declared and nothing is chosen.** `src/transfer/readout_depth.py` declares `DEPTH_PROJECTION_SEED = 20261123` and `DEPTH_PROJECTION_DIM = 256`, and `project_summaries` fixes the rule: each block's seed is `base_seed + 8 * depth_index + the summary's index in the declared order ('mean', 'last', 'mut', 'suffix')`. The reassessment's own selection record carries `projection_seed: 20261123` and `coordinates: 256` for **all ten** of its cells, sourced from `docs/D1_READOUT_DEPTH_SWEEP.md`, section *Per-arm depth selection*. The rule reserves eight seeds per depth and depends only on the depth index and the summary index, never on the cohort, so the selected depth k uses `20261123 + 8k` and `20261123 + 8k + 1` and an admitted depth j uses the same rule at j. Both arms of the contrast therefore come from one declared projection, and taking the admitted arm from the admitted *extraction* instead would have let two projections differ and reintroduced the confound this design removes.

**The inputs are verified and the form is fixed.** Each gate's every-block wave archive holds, per background, one `features_d<NNN>` array per hooked depth shaped (rows, 2, hidden width) — the `mean` and `last` summaries in the declared order — beside the admitted `projected` block, and the manifests report `status: complete` with every block index hooked. So a depth's representation is assembled without re-extracting anything: project each of the two summaries to 256 coordinates with `readout_depth.project_summaries` at `base_seed + 8 * depth + the summary's declared index`, concatenate to 512, and form the same mutation difference the gate's own loader forms, `projected[state] - projected[0]`, in the cohort's own variant order.

**No width check is bypassed after all.** The plan had been to relax `require_projected_width` for this contrast. It is not necessary and not done: the gate's loader is called unchanged, its own 1,024-coordinate `projected` block passes the check, and the 512-coordinate depth block replaces `R` afterwards. Everything downstream — folds, control sets, ridge grid, resampling unit, contrast form — is the gate's own, called through its own module.

**The seven arms and their depths**, identical on every cohort because the selection is per arm: ProGen3-112M 9, ProGen3-3B 11, ProtGPT2 21, ProGen2-medium 23, ProGen2-large 25, ProGen2-xlarge 28, ProteinGLM-7B-CLM 30. Each arm's admitted pair comes from its own gate manifest — ProGen2-large 15 and 31 on the stability cohort, for instance — and the contrast runs the selected depth against those two and against evenly spread others, six to eight blocks per arm, all at 512 coordinates. One check rides along in every cell: the baseline predictions must be identical across the depths of an arm at a given seed, since nothing but `R` changes, and the report records that comparison rather than assuming it.

### The depth contrast on three cohorts, and what it establishes

The contrast ran on all three reconciled cohorts: seven arms each, 45 blocks each, three split seeds, **405 fits in total, no failures**. The depth-invariance control — with only the representation block varying, everything that does not depend on it must be identical across an arm's depths — holds on all three, and **the three do not check it the same way, so the count is given per cohort rather than summed**. Folding stability and remote homology checksum the baseline predictions themselves: **135 of 135** on each. The residue-interaction adapter never computed that checksum, so no such count exists for it; the equivalent was derived afterwards from its persisted artefacts, where every contrast whose augmented design does not carry R must be unchanged across depths and every depth must carry the same fold identity — **2,025 contrast comparisons at a largest absolute difference of 0.0 and 135 fold-identity comparisons, no mismatches**. An earlier report of this as "405 of 405" asserted a single control on a cohort where it had not been run, and is corrected here.

Each cell is the selected depth's mean increment minus the better admitted depth's, over three seeds, in that gate's own units — kcal²/mol² of group-equal held-out error reduced on folding stability and residue interactions, and the gate's own increment scale on remote homology — so the columns are read for sign and rank rather than compared in magnitude.

**The sign of these increments separates the cohorts far more sharply than their ordering does.** Of each cohort's 135 fitted depth-and-seed points, the share falling below zero is **7.4% on folding stability, 11.1% on remote homology and 63.7% on residue interactions**. At 512 coordinates the representation reduces group-equal held-out error at most depths on the first two cohorts and increases it at most depths on the third, six to nine times as often. That makes the residue-interaction column a different kind of quantity rather than a noisier version of the same one, and it is the reason its arm ordering is read with its resolved-seed counts attached.

**The selection does not transfer as a property of the depth.** Over the five arms that produce a comparison, the selected depth beats both admitted depths on **four** for folding stability and on **one** for each of the other two cohorts. **No arm is above on all three**, and one — ProGen2-large — is below on all three. The two arms whose selection landed on an already-admitted depth produce no difference on any cohort, which is why the comparison population is five and not seven.

**What that licenses, and what it does not.** It licenses the scope statement: a depth chosen because it resolves on the readout panel is not thereby the depth carrying the most transferable information on another endpoint, and on two of three cohorts it usually loses to the depths the admitted design already pools. It does **not** license a claim that the selection is wrong, because these are 512-coordinate designs against a published 1,024, unadjusted over 45 blocks per gate, on one axis. And the same fact shows up in all three cohorts independently of the selection: the highest-reading depth is an unselected, unadmitted block on four to five of seven arms in each, so the admitted pair is not the ceiling either.

**Two populations exist in every arm panel of this contrast, and a count must say which it is over.** Seven arms are measured per gate; only those whose selected depth differs from both admitted depths produce a selected-minus-admitted difference — five on each cohort so far, because two arms' selections landed on a depth the admitted extraction already hooked. A count over arms measured and a count over arms that produce a comparison are different quantities, and a difference statistic exists only for the second. This is the same failure as a zero quoted without its denominator and as a count over declared metrics read as a count over data, and it has now happened a third time: a resolved-at-all-three-seeds count was reported as five on folding stability, which is neither its comparison-population value of four nor its all-arms value of six. Each count in these tables carries its population.

**What it is and is not.** It is a new measurement and it is **not comparable to the published 1,024-coordinate increment**: a 512-coordinate design and a 1,024-coordinate one differ in capacity, so their increments cannot be set beside each other, and the published verdict is untouched by whatever this returns. The question it does answer — does the selected depth carry more than an admitted depth, at equal capacity, under the same projection? — is the one the selection actually raises. A width-forced replay would have answered neither, conflating depth with capacity.

## Deferred: three sibling stages, three argument semantics

The three fit stages behind the four gates disagree on three arguments, and a caller cannot tell which convention applies from any interface it can read. Scheduled like the dispatch-layer items — deferred with its reason while campaigns are in flight, with what would close it.

| Argument | `fit_stability_singles.py` | `fit_nested_singles.py` | `fit_pairwise_epistasis.py` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `--extraction` | the arm's own directory | the arm's own directory | the **parent**; the stage appends the arm |
| BLAS threads | free, accepts `--threads 8` | **pinned to 4**, refuses otherwise | free |
| `--out` | a **directory** | a **directory** | a **file path** |

**Measured cost so far: two relaunches and 33 discarded fits.** And the three are not equally expensive, which is the part worth carrying: the extraction-path and thread-pin differences refuse at the **input** stage and cost only a relaunch, while the `--out` difference fails at the **output** stage — each of the 33 pairwise fits ran to completion and then threw `IsADirectoryError` at its final write. So **where in a run an interface difference surfaces determines what it costs**, and an argument name carries no indication of which meaning it has. A difference that fails late is worth fixing ahead of one that fails early, even when the early one looks more confusing.

The thread asymmetry is the one where both behaviours are defensible: the pin exists because BLAS thread count sets a float32 reduction order, which cost the class sweep its first C0 gate, and it is load-bearing for a stage forming a wide random projection in a way it is not for one forming a mutation difference — this work's own evidence being a stability replay at eight threads reproducing its published fit at 1e-13 over 919 numbers. What is missing is not the pin but the statement of where it applies.

**What closes it:** the three stages agreeing on argument semantics, or each documenting where it differs and why. **Why deferred:** a refactor across three production fit stages with four campaigns in flight is not a change to make today, and the failures are loud rather than silent.

## The dispatch layer: two deferred fixes and one accepted property

Three findings about the campaign dispatch layer came out of this work. They share an owner and a blocker — campaigns are running through that layer — so they are recorded together rather than as three notes in three documents. **Two are deferred work, scheduled for when the queues drain rather than declined. The third is not work at all**: it is an accepted design property whose cost has been measured, and filing it beside the fixes would invite someone to reclaim it. None of the three is a correctness defect in a published number.

**The skip-identity check** (L54). A dispatched cell is skipped when an artefact merely *exists* at the path its label derives, never when that artefact is the one this cell would have produced, so two launches whose labels coincide silently resume each other and both report complete under a clean tally. The fix is designed: the skip should require the existing artefact's own `identity` block to name the arm and the plan digest of the cell it would skip, which every extractor already writes, so the check has its material. Deferred because tightening skip semantics while three campaigns run through the layer could turn another agent's legitimate resume into a refusal. What closes it: that one condition at the skip, with its own validation pass. What bounds it meanwhile: every campaign builder now carries a discriminator in its labels, and all eight depth-all manifests use four distinct cohort tags.

**Receipts should carry the thread count and the host.** A published cell records its device, its torch and NumPy versions and every input digest, and neither the thread count nor the host, which is exactly why the crossed-control replay's 1.58e-6 to 2.94e-6 departure cannot be attributed. Together with the function-granularity resampler digest L55 names, these turn such a departure from an unattributable difference into a named difference in reduction order — and the eigendecomposition reason means it will recur on any design carrying a wide random projection. Deferred because it changes what every stage writes, and a receipt field added mid-campaign would split the panel into cells that carry it and cells that do not. What closes it: two fields at the point the receipt is assembled, added once across the stages rather than per gate.

**The slot barrier leaves cards idle — and that is an accepted property, not a deferred fix.** Slot N+1 starts only when every cell of slot N has exited, so a slot's longest cell holds the others' cards. Measured while the waves ran: 3 of 4 cards idle at 4 MiB on one pod while a single slot-4 cell finished, with 16 cells still pending behind it. The barrier is what makes a slot's cells a set that either all completed or did not, and that is what every tally in this layer rests on; trading it for per-card dispatch buys throughput with the property the tallies are read through. **It looks like free throughput and is not.** Nothing here is waiting to be done: the cost is recorded so that a later reader can see it was measured and kept, rather than overlooked. Anyone who does want per-card dispatch owes the slot's completion semantics first, and owes them when nothing is in flight.

## Limitations

The driver enforces identity of support, folds, seeds, weighting, label budget and resampling contract; it does not and cannot enforce that a recomputed increment means the same thing as the published one, which is a matter of the readout class and belongs to each gate's own reading. A recomputed increment is as bounded as the published one it replaces: by the declared controls, the label budget, the cohort, and now by the selected class and depth rather than the admitted ones.

The pipeline-identity tolerance of 1e-8 absolute is the tolerance the admitted admission receipt used for the same comparison. It bounds the refit's agreement with the published predictions and certifies nothing about numerical accuracy against a higher-precision reference. The per-assay blocking and the pinned thread count make a cell reproducible on its own terms; a refit on a different host or thread budget is not guaranteed bit-identical, and the class sweep's own measurement — that a perturbation three orders of magnitude larger moves no reported correlation — is what bounds the consequence rather than a proof of invariance.

**On the depth axis the published side of a comparison may not exist yet.** The depth sweep retained no fitted per-block estimate: its cells carry plans, digests, declared axes and reproduction controls and no estimate of any kind, so a re-run with retention is what will produce the published side. A cell whose published report is absent is refused at plan time, before any array is read, and that refusal is the correct outcome: the number in a record is not a published artefact, and reading it as one would compare a recomputation against prose.

The retention declaration is a transcription of what each gate record states it retained. A location that is declared and, when probed on the allocation, absent is a finding the probe produces; a location that is present is present, and the probe does not certify that its contents are the arrays the published fit read. That check belongs to the loader, which verifies the cohort digest, the manifest identity, each archive's checksum and metadata, the exact mutation order, the measured effects and the profile scores before a single row is fitted.

The contract's element declarations are read-only and digest-bound to the records they transcribe, so they cannot drift unnoticed; they are not a second source for any quantity, and a reader wanting a gate's numbers reads that gate's record.
