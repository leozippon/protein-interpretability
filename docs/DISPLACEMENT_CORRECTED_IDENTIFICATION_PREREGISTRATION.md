# Pre-registration — a displacement-corrected per-arm identification rule

**Date:** 2026-08-23 **Status:** frozen before the comparison is computed; asserts no result. **Scope:** whether the fixed identification constant `budget.SCREENING_CONTEXT_INFORMATION_NATS = 0.05` should be replaced by a per-arm rule read off a Jensen-displacement-corrected bootstrap interval. **Authority:** `docs/INTERPRETABILITY_TRANSFER_AUDIT.md` stays canonical for findings and limitations. `docs/CONTEXT_INFORMATION_UNCERTAINTY_PREREGISTRATION.md` stays canonical for the estimand `I = H_baseline − H_model` and the group-level paired bootstrap; `docs/MEASURABILITY_THRESHOLD_CALIBRATION_PREREGISTRATION.md` stays canonical for the null family, the λ family, the two objectives and the selection rule this plan reuses without restating.

**What the campaign did, recorded 2026-08-23 (EXP-R2-221) and not folded into the text below.** The plan executed as frozen and **all three adoption conditions hold**, so the corrected per-arm rule replaces the fixed constant as the identification criterion. Two things are worth naming here rather than leaving to a reader. The declared sensitivity — holding the reference fixed instead of correcting it — **fails** the same target at 9 of 120 held-out null readings and 3 of 56 distinct measurements, which is what separates a repaired interval from a narrower one and is why that candidate was declared a sensitivity rather than an alternative. And the adoption is **not** total: this plan's own rule requires an interval, and the stage that produces the statistics an interval is computed from runs before one exists, so `budget.SCREENING_CONTEXT_INFORMATION_NATS` survives at those sites as a pre-interval screen rather than becoming inert everywhere. That is a departure from what "replace the constant" implied when this was written, it is a property of the pipeline rather than of the criterion, and §5.10(f) of the audit carries it. The results are in that section; nothing is admitted to this file.

## Why this exists

EXP-R2-218 split one undeclared constant into two criteria and EXP-R2-219 cross-fitted the survivor. Identification became a point rule on a fixed constant, `Î ≥ 0.05`; ratio admissibility became the per-arm, precision-referenced condition `Î > 8.7652·SE(Î)`. Neither is a function of the other, and EXP-R2-220 found the first pair of real readings on which they cross in opposite directions:

| reading | `Î` | SE | `Î`/SE | 0.05 floor | Fieller |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| `galactica-1.3b` protein | +0.047678 | 0.004571 | 10.43 | **FAIL** by 0.0023 nats | PASS |
| `Llama-2-7b-hf` protein | +0.084287 | 0.010179 | 8.28 | PASS | **FAIL** |

A reading ten standard errors from zero is refused as unidentified, and a reading eight standard errors from zero is admitted. That is what a magnitude rule and a precision rule do when the standard error spans a factor of 2.2 between two arms, and it is a demonstrable false negative of the fixed constant rather than a fact about either checkpoint.

The reason the per-arm interval rule was not adopted at EXP-R2-218 is measured and specific. It lost on error rate: its calibrated threshold was 0.020 nats and cross-fitted it reached 0.0583 out of sample under two of three schemes. The cause is catalogued as **L34/L42**, the **Jensen displacement**: the bootstrap refits the smoothed unigram on each resampled reference, `−log` is convex, so the drawn baselines sit above the full-reference value and the whole interval is lifted off the point estimate it surrounds — by a mean of +0.0095 and a maximum of +0.0347 nats at the null, entirely in the baseline term, ordered along the `V/R` axis. Fifty-six of 112 null lower bounds sit above zero before any signal exists, and removing the displacement takes the interval rule's calibrated threshold from 0.020 to 0.000: its whole apparent floor is displacement.

**The hypothesis frozen here.** If the displacement is removed, the interval rule's known defect is removed with it, a per-arm identification rule controls its false-positive rate at a true zero, the crossing disappears because identification and admissibility become nested, and `galactica-1.3b`'s protein mode is admitted on its own evidence rather than refused by a constant.

## The correction, chosen from the diagnosed mechanism

The displacement is a property of one resampling step: refitting `q̂` on a resampled reference. It is therefore measured directly rather than modelled. For each draw `d` the bootstrap already evaluates every statistic at `(cohort draw d, reference draw d)`. This plan adds a second evaluation at `(cohort draw d, full reference)` — the same cohort draw, the reference held at the sample actually observed — and defines, per statistic `S`,

`D_S = mean_d [ S(c_d, r_d) − S(c_d, r_full) ]`.

`D_S` is the mean displacement attributable to reference resampling alone, with the cohort draw differenced out. The **displacement-corrected interval** is the percentile interval of `S(c_d, r_d) − D_S`. The point estimate is untouched, the standard error is untouched, and the reference's contribution to the width is untouched; only the interval's position moves, which is the only thing the diagnosis says is wrong.

Three properties are asserted in advance as conditions that must hold, not as results. `D` for `information_nats_per_token` must equal `D` for `baseline_entropy_nats_per_token`, because the model term does not depend on the reference. `D` must be non-negative, by Jensen. `D` must vanish when the reference cannot be resampled into anything else, which is the degenerate single-group case.

**Why not the alternatives.** *An analytic correction* — the second-order term `½ Σ_v p_c(v)·Var(q̂*(v))/q̂(v)²`, with its known `V/R` scaling — matches the same mechanism, but it is a truncated expansion whose neglected terms grow with exactly the quantity being corrected, so it is least trustworthy on the arms where the correction is largest. The measured displacement above is exact in the bootstrap world and needs no expansion; the analytic form is retained only as the reason the correction is expected to order along `V/R`. *Holding the reference fixed altogether* removes the displacement by removing the term, and with it the reference's genuine contribution to the width; §5.08(c) already reports that this takes the interval rule's threshold to zero, which is uninterpretable while the standard error is also shrinking. It is evaluated here as a declared sensitivity and not as a candidate: the artefact will carry the cohort-only standard error and the reference-held-fixed interval beside the corrected one, so that "the corrected rule passes because its interval is narrower" can be checked rather than argued. *A studentized or double bootstrap* addresses scale and coverage, not location; the diagnosed defect is a location displacement, and an inner bootstrap is disproportionate machinery for it. *A BC or BCa percentile interval* is the textbook remedy for a displaced bootstrap distribution and is rejected for a narrower reason: `z₀` conflates the reference displacement with genuine skewness of the cohort bootstrap, so applying it would move every interval for reasons the diagnosis does not name. This package has no BCa and does not acquire one here.

## The rule proposed for adoption

**`IDENTIFIED` iff the displacement-corrected 95% interval for `I` lies strictly above zero.**

No constant. It is the per-arm, precision-referenced form of the identification question, approximately `Î > 1.96·SE(Î)`, and it is by construction weaker than the ratio-admissibility condition `Î > 8.7652·SE(Î)`, so the two become nested rather than crossing. Where no bootstrap interval exists the rule is **not evaluable and must say so**, in the same shape and for the same reason `budget.ratio_denominator_admissibility` already refuses a denominator with no standard error. It must not silently fall back to a point comparison.

## How it is evaluated

Unchanged from EXP-R2-219 in every respect except the statistic being scored, so that the comparison is between two rules on one set of readings. The unigram-predictor null control (`information_bootstrap.unigram_null_control`), 15 arms × 8 blocks = **120 readings**, which are **56 distinct measurements** over **24 cohort draws** and **seven tokenisation classes**, at seed **20260820** and **2000** draws over the per-record sufficient statistics under `results/transfer/ctx_uncertainty_20260821/`. CPU only; no model is loaded and no cluster is reached.

Leave-one-arm-out remains invalid and is not run: a control's reading is a property of the block's records and the arm's vocabulary alone, so eleven of fifteen arms share a reading and a held-out arm would leave copies of itself in the derivation. The three schemes are EXP-R2-219's: **leave one cohort draw out** (24 folds), **the same buffered against the control rotation** (24 folds), and **leave one tokenisation class out** (7 folds). On each fold a threshold is re-derived from the calibration units alone by the same selection rule on the same 0.005-nat grid and scored on the held-out units, beside the shipped 0.05 floor, the shipped Fieller condition, and the corrected rule at zero. Rates are reported over all three unit counts with one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson upper limits, as EXP-R2-219 reported them.

## The adoption criterion, fixed before the comparison

The corrected per-arm rule replaces `SCREENING_CONTEXT_INFORMATION_NATS` as the operative identification criterion **only if all three hold**.

**A1 — it controls its error rate out of sample.** Under every one of the three fold schemes, the held-out false-positive rate of the corrected rule at zero is **at most 0.05 counted over readings and at most 0.05 counted over distinct measurements**. Both counts, because §5.09 established that the 120 readings are 56 measurements and a rate over the larger count is the more flattering one. The rate over the 24 cohort draws is reported with its Clopper-Pearson limit and is deliberately **not** made a pass condition: 24 units cannot resolve 0.05 at all — one false positive is already 0.042 and two is 0.083 — so a criterion on that count would be decided by a single reading, which is the failure EXP-R2-219 diagnosed in the calibrated 0.010.

**A2 — it resolves the crossing.** On the fifteen panel arms across all eight blocks and on the fourteen stage-21 cells, no reading is admissible as a ratio denominator while unidentified. Equivalently the two criteria are nested with Fieller the strictly stronger one, so no pair of readings can be ordered one way by identification and the other way by admissibility.

**A3 — nothing currently admitted is lost.** No reading admitted by the shipped 0.05 floor is refused by the corrected rule, over the 120 real-arm panel readings and the fourteen stage-21 cells.

**A4 — the instrument is sound.** A halt condition rather than an outcome. The correction must leave every point estimate and every standard error bit-identical; `D` must be non-negative, must equal the baseline term's displacement, and must reproduce the `V/R` ladder §5.08(c) reports — largest on the arms with the largest vocabulary against reference size, at most a rounding error on the residue-level arms. If any of these fails the campaign reports an instrument defect and decides nothing.

**If A1, A2 or A3 fails, no gate changes.** The crossing is then recorded as a named limitation in the audit catalogue with the exact numbers, `galactica-1.3b`'s refusal is stated as a known false negative of a deliberately conservative constant rather than as an evidenced non-identification, and what would be required to remove it is written down. No adoption is manufactured and this criterion is not revised after the numbers exist.

## What is genuinely blind here, and what is not

The displacement's size at the null is published — mean +0.0095, maximum +0.0347 nats — so the *direction* of the correction's effect on the interval rule is known, and §5.08(c) already reports that removing it takes the calibrated threshold to 0.000. What is not known, and what this campaign measures, is the corrected rule's **held-out** false-positive rate: a threshold of zero derived in sample says nothing about the rate on units the derivation never saw, and EXP-R2-219 showed exactly that gap opening under a rule whose in-sample rate met its target.

One structural reservation is declared in advance because it bears on how the answer must be read whichever way it comes out. The null control's true value is zero **in expectation over its borrowed reference**: conditional on the particular independent block whose unigram the control predicts with, the control's true information is a small random offset around zero rather than exactly zero. The bootstrap resamples the block's own reference and not the borrowed one, so the standard error it reports for a control omits one of the two sources of variability that scatter the control's true value. The consequence is signed and is not symmetric between the rules being compared: it inflates the corrected interval rule's measured false-positive rate relative to the rate that rule would have at an exactly-zero arm, and leaves the point rule at 0.05 untouched, because 0.05 is far outside the scatter either way. If the corrected rule fails A1, that reservation is part of the diagnosis and the campaign must say how large the effect is rather than invoking it; the artefact therefore also carries the cohort-only standard error, from which the reference term's share of the variance is read directly.
