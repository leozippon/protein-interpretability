# Predictive information beyond sequence-statistical controls

This is a design and feasibility draft, not a frozen experiment. The user has authorized completion and validation of the full [readout diagnostic](D1_READOUT_PROTOCOL.md), followed by a bounded pairwise/epistasis experiment. Readout admission remains the execution gate; no new model inference, baseline training or biological outcome analysis has been launched for this draft. The intended D1 paper combines capability comparisons with Readout and one or two discriminating hierarchy tests. D2 applies existing methods to an established capability; D3 develops a method only after an existing method's limitation has been demonstrated.

The operational question is: on a specified biological endpoint and held-out support, does adding a model score or representation improve prediction beyond a declared baseline with the same experimental-label budget? This asks about accessible predictive information under the fitted predictor. It does not assume encoded biological knowledge, equate a likelihood score with knowledge, or infer an internal mechanism from a residual association. The [canonical audit](INTERPRETABILITY_TRANSFER_AUDIT.md) owns admitted findings and withdrawals; no withdrawn Stage-46 ProGen3 score enters this plan.

## Three axes rather than one ladder

Composition, local motifs, family profiles, pairwise couplings, higher-order dependencies and structure/function constraints are useful families of alternatives, but they do not form a strict total order.

| Alternative | What a specified control can express | What it does not identify by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Composition and length | Residue frequencies, mutation identities, broad sequence composition | Sequence arrangement or a particular biochemical rule |
| Local sequence statistics | Adjacent residue combinations or bounded-window patterns, with the window explicitly stated | Long-range dependence; a matching motif is not evidence of motif function |
| Family/profile statistics | Position-dependent residue frequencies, gaps and conservation conditional on an alignment or family | Explicit fitted pair couplings; the family assignment and corpus can already carry structural and functional associations |
| Pairwise dependence | Couplings between two sites, with local and distant site pairs distinguished | Third-order interaction terms in the specified statistical representation, or measured double-mutant epistasis |
| Higher-order dependence | Residual interactions beyond a declared lower-order model under a fixed scale and conditioning set | Biological relevance, a particular physical mechanism or direct experimental validation |
| Structure/function constraints | Prediction of independently measured structure-associated or functional outcomes | A uniquely structural computation; simpler statistics can predict the same outcomes |

Dependency range concerns distance between sequence positions. Interaction order concerns how many variables enter an irreducible term under a specified representation. Biological relevance concerns an independently defined measured endpoint. A distant pair can be second order; an adjacent triplet can be third order. A motif can be functional while a long-range dependence is not predictive of the assay. A position-independent mixture of family profiles can induce apparent dependencies when family identity is unobserved. Nonlinear readouts of low-order features can also produce higher-order output behavior. Accordingly, label each implemented control by its actual operations, data and conditioning; do not assign it a biological sophistication level solely from its name.

## Estimands and data regimes

Keep three questions separate. First, phenotype prediction asks whether model outputs improve held-family prediction of measured mutant effects after controls. Second, sequence-model approximation asks how well controls reproduce model scores on natural sequences and declared perturbations. Third, generation asks which constraints newly sampled sequences satisfy relative to matched statistical generators. Strong agreement in the second question does not establish the answer to the first or third. None of the three alone identifies how a model computes its output.

A no-context model receives the target sequence only. An external-profile control additionally receives a searched sequence corpus and query-dependent homolog selection. That is a valid practical baseline, but it is not a matched-information training comparison. A supplied-homolog model is a separate conditional regime; compare it with controls built from the same supplied information, alongside the explicitly broader external-profile baseline. Unconditional generation has no requested family, and cannot be compared as though a family-conditioned generator solved the same task. Post-hoc assignment of generated sequences to families is a selection step whose unassigned outcomes must remain in the denominator.

Within each baseline comparison, match assay and variant support, label budgets, family splits, target transformations, fitting/tuning budget and evaluation weighting. Bind external sequence resources, search results and baseline parameters to source hashes; use the same corpus when comparing local-statistic and profile baselines trained from homologs. Model pretraining exposure cannot generally be matched retrospectively and remains a limitation. Exact target copies, near relatives, annotation provenance, search truncation and profile depth must be reported, not interpreted as certified pretraining absence.

### Targets, controls and label access

On the verified stability scale define epsilon = y_AB − y_A − y_B + y_WT. Experimental single-mutant measurements used to construct this target are not automatically predictor inputs. Primary prediction receives sequences and frozen sequence-derived features only; held-out group WT/single/double labels enter target construction and evaluation, never fitted feature scaling, baseline selection or model tuning. An optional singles-assisted task may supply measured WT and singles to every comparator, with its label budget and task name declared separately. Because those measurements also enter epsilon with signed coefficients, shared noise creates mechanical correlations; such a secondary task requires independent measurement replicates or an explicit validated noise-aware model, and is not part of the smallest primary experiment. It cannot be reported as zero-shot experimental-label generalization.

Compare the following matched predictors on the same cycles and folds:

| Control or augmentation | Required operation |
| --- | --- |
| Zero interaction / additive phenotype | Predict epsilon = 0; this has a defined squared error but undefined Spearman, which must be reported as undefined |
| Sequence and profile baseline C | Mutation identities, positions/separation, composition/local-pattern changes and WT/single profile summaries; fit only on training groups |
| Nonlinear-additive nuisance G | A prespecified monotone/global response model of additive sequence/profile or training-predicted single effects, with calibration fitted exclusively on training groups; tune capacity in inner folds |
| Pairwise sequence baseline Q | Regularized pairwise alignment model and independent-site control from the same frozen homolog rows, using no phenotype labels for sequence-model fitting or coupling selection |
| Model likelihood interaction | M_AB − M_A − M_B + M_WT, with native scoring convention and tokenization declared |
| Frozen representation augmentation | R_AB − R_A − R_B + R_WT, using the declared middle/final mean/last-token pools and fixed projection, plus constituent first-order representation differences as a separate control |

An independent-site log profile has zero double contrast by construction for aligned substitutions. Do not manufacture a nonzero pairwise comparator or a Spearman score for it: its constituent-site features and nonlinear calibration are the relevant stronger nuisance controls. A model representation double contrast can improve prediction through nonlinear sequence encoding; the observation alone does not locate a pairwise mechanism. Comparing the interaction contrast against the constituent first-order representation features helps distinguish an added contrast from simply supplying richer single-mutant information. BPE token boundaries, token counts and mean-pooling denominators can generate nonzero representation cycles even at distant amino-acid sites. Record per-state token counts and their cycle, position shifts and segmentation changes; include these inexpensive interface descriptors in the matched baseline and report amino-acid/byte/BPE strata separately. Distance ≥10 does not eliminate this artifact, and descriptor adjustment does not certify that all tokenization effects were removed. A biological higher-order claim cannot follow from a pooled-representation increment alone.

The existing searched corpus rows can be reconstructed with `src/transfer/epistasis.py:alignment_rows`; retained profile arrays alone are insufficient for a pairwise fit. No ready official alignment panel for the new MegaScale cohort was established in this inventory. Local Swiss-Prot FASTA/XML and residue-level Pfam annotations exist, and the repository already provides DIAMOND search, alignment-row reconstruction and profile estimation. The retained MegaScale hit table is a prior retrieval certificate, not an admitted coupling alignment. `scripts/transfer/run_s48_annotate_native.sh` documents previously staged H200 assets under `data/r233_annotation_assets/` (DIAMOND, HMMER, pressed Pfam-A and a UniRef50 database); their current availability and hashes require a read-only remote check before planning reuse. No ready MMseqs or regularized DCA executable was established by the bounded local inventory. WT_cluster construction was not independently source-verified here, so a source label cannot substitute for a validated grouping contract. Thus homolog mapping, coverage and a defensible pairwise baseline are blocking steps for a claim of information beyond pairwise sequence statistics. The former bias-corrected MI/APC channel failed its DMS diagnostic on 0/22 eligible assays; it must not be renamed as a strong pairwise upper bound. A changed regularized estimator needs label-independent held-out-sequence fit checks against its own independent-site baseline, with depths and failures reported. Passing that check establishes a usable sequence comparator, not biological correctness. If support remains inadequate, narrow the claim to additive/nonlinear-additive controls and retain the pairwise boundary as unresolved.

Use nested held-group folds, equal group/background weighting, training-only preprocessing and identical labels/support for every incremental comparison. Retain three prespecified split seeds and the fixed projection seed from Readout, but regenerate folds on the new frozen group universe. Fit linear model augmentations with the existing ridge recipe; allow the nuisance comparator its explicitly prespecified nonlinear calibration so that linear baseline underfitting is not mislabeled as higher-order information. Neither calibration nor target residualization may be fitted to held-out family double labels. Assay-specific nonlinearity that cannot transfer across groups remains a limitation, not permission to calibrate on the test outcomes.

Primary evaluation is paired reduction in group-equal epsilon mean squared error relative to the strongest admitted nuisance/control set, in squared units of the verified stability scale, accompanied by within-background Spearman and paired increments for nonconstant predictors. Compare adding likelihood and adding representation separately, including gains after Q when Q is admitted. Use group-bootstrap intervals, report all split seeds, and account for reused WT/single measurements when quantifying target uncertainty; source fit intervals are not independent biological replicates. Do not set a negligible-information cutoff after observing results. An unresolved increment does not prove absent biological information, and a positive increment is bounded by the declared controls, label budget and readout class.

### Minimal nuisance implementation and compute estimate

An executable nuisance candidate is a first-stage ridge predictor of absolute WT/single stability from sequence/profile features, fitted only on training groups, followed by an isotonic calibration of its additive predictions. Generate cross-fitted first-stage predictions within the outer training set before fitting the calibration; use measured training WT/singles/doubles with the same declared label budget available to all supervised comparators. For each held-out cycle construct the additive double prediction from predicted singles and predicted WT, apply the same training-fitted calibration to all four states, and form the resulting calibrated cycle. This permits a global monotone response to induce apparent interactions without supplying a fitted pair interaction. Compare it with the uncalibrated additive null and the directly fitted sequence/profile predictor. All first-stage regularization and calibration choices belong inside the nested training procedure. This is a concrete proposed nuisance class, not every possible global-epistasis model; a failure to explain a residual does not rule out assay-specific measurement effects.

The provisional short-sequence panel is practical for all 33 unconditioned Readout checkpoints. Fresh H200 FP32 singleton qualification measured about 0.200 seconds per sequence for Galactica-30B at 39 packed tokens and 0.151 seconds for Qwen2.5-32B at 22 tokens, counting seven forwards with representation processing and repeats, after loading. Median-length qualification gave about 0.407 and 0.325 seconds per sequence respectively. Applying 0.2–0.4 seconds to 17,822 short sequences suggests roughly 1–2 GPU-hours for each largest arm, excluding loading; this is a planning extrapolation from three smoke assays per arm, not a benchmark interval. A naive extrapolation of the earlier 29-arm qualification mixture gives about 14 GPU-hours total for those arms, but mixes much longer sequences, batching and startup effects. Budget roughly 10–25 GPU-hours for full-panel extraction as an explicitly provisional planning range, then replace it with a short label-blind throughput qualification before launch. Model loading, precision repeats, new-profile/pairwise fitting and nested readouts are additional costs. With five usable GPUs this is a several-hour extraction workload, subject to large-arm scheduling, rather than an assured completion time. Keep each model loaded across backgrounds and deduplicate the four-state sequences; extra inference replicas should be justified by the measured critical path.

This does not identify absolute epsilon sign/magnitude, establish an additive biological scale, or remove global assay nonlinearity. Any such secondary panel must state this narrower estimand and keep assay-specific calibration labels separate from test labels. ## What later contrasts would identify

Existing epistasis work constrains this plan. EXP-R2-176–180 and the completed D3.d attainability run in the [experiment log](EXPERIMENT_LOG.md) already examined a measured-double-mutant cohort spanning 51 assays and 48 identity clusters. The frozen corpus-derived coupling channel passed its own construction-matched null on none of 22 eligible assays and was declared unusable; many raw corpus hits did not guarantee adequate effective information for estimating joint residue frequencies. A separate planted-repeat instrument passed on the text control and failed on both tested protein arms, with a tokenizer confound for ProtGPT2. D3.d Stage 2 was stopped. Those are binding findings about those instruments, not evidence that all protein-model interactions are absent. The new direction does not repeat or automatically reopen that stopped probe. Any successor must change the failed measurement or baseline condition and state the narrower new claim.

That feasibility work also found that removing a fitted global assay nonlinearity reduced pair-level spread substantially in several inspected assays. Thus global nonlinearity is a required alternative, not optional cleanup. The retained GB1 four-site and dense-double panels are possible bounded phenotype-model comparisons, but the local files have no explicit measured WT row. A fitted intercept permits an additive-versus-interacting predictor comparison; it does not manufacture a directly measured WT-centered epistasis cycle. Exact matching lower-order variants, replicate uncertainty, score direction/scale and an independently validated WT baseline require admission before such cycles are interpreted. A single GB1 assay/family cannot establish a boundary across protein families.

A controlled sequence perturbation can preserve composition while changing order, preserve exact k-mer counts while changing longer arrangements, or preserve a family's position-wise frequencies across a sequence ensemble while changing couplings. Each design must verify the statistics it preserves and changes, and retain all failed matches. Profile-column preservation is generally an ensemble constraint, not a claim about each shuffled individual sequence. A likelihood difference on such a contrast demonstrates sensitivity to the altered sequence properties; it does not show functional relevance or prove that only the intended dependency changed. Perturbations can also move sequences outside the natural distribution. These experiments supplement measured phenotype prediction rather than replace it.

A pairwise family model is a useful substantive comparator only if a changed data/estimation interface overcomes the existing unusable-coupling result. Use the same alignment/corpus and query support as its independent-site control, separate nearby from distant pairs, and keep coupling selection and regularization independent of test phenotype labels. Variation in homolog depth or model fit quality must not be mistaken for a dependency-order boundary. A pairwise campaign is deferred until this data sufficiency check and its concrete protocol are complete.

Measured epistasis requires matched WT, both single mutants and their double mutant in the same assay and condition. On a declared phenotype scale, compare the double-mutant effect with the additive prediction from its constituent singles, propagate measurement uncertainty, and compare models against a fitted global-nonlinearity control. Nonadditivity of an arbitrary transformed assay score is not by itself a molecular interaction. A model's double-mutant likelihood residual, distant pair coupling, or score on a scrambled sequence is a different quantity. Higher-order biological interactions require the corresponding experimentally measured combinatorial panels and a declared lower-order null.

Structure/function claims require independent labels or controlled experimental measurements. Profile recognition and predictor confidence are proxies and cannot be promoted to folding/function validation. If sequence-statistical controls predict the same biological endpoint, that does not make the endpoint nonbiological; it limits what model-specific information and mechanisms the comparison establishes.
