# Label instrument for measured double-mutant nonadditivity: qualification results

This is the measurement-only qualification that the [pairwise protocol](D1_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY_PROTOCOL.md) requires before any full-panel model extraction. It builds the WT-centered cycle epsilon = y_AB − y_A − y_B + y_WT separately from the trypsin and the chymotrypsin channel of the pinned MegaScale dataset2 bytes, then asks whether these labels carry reproducible interaction signal and what scope that licenses. No model score, likelihood or representation enters any quantity below, so the decision recorded here cannot be tuned to a model outcome. The protocol owns the design, the target definition and the comparator set; this document owns the measured reliability of the labels and the resulting scope.

The analysis ran on Compute in the `ct` environment on 2026-09-24T01:20:26 UTC as `scripts/transfer/qualify_megascale_label_instrument.py --out logs/d1_pairwise_label_instrument_20260923` with 2,000 group-bootstrap resamples and seed 20260923. It verifies both staged parquet files against the pinned manifest digests before reading them. Raw outputs are the ignored `logs/d1_pairwise_label_instrument_20260923/qualification.json` (SHA-256 `e33d614a93f6f47f15f2c093925928b3b43bac9e5e6a90b439a8a603742c3e07`) and `backgrounds.json` (SHA-256 `04c93e22fca29838a80ed14aaf7d59c5c17be73a6e72bf7a04a7b518b0cca3ed`).

## Decision

The label instrument is admitted, on a support that differs from the pre-registered one by one added rule, and with a scope narrower than the protocol's provisional counts imply.

Three conditions were checked before the decision. Between-channel agreement had to have a group-bootstrap interval bounded away from zero; the reproducible component had to be large relative to the per-channel discordance rather than merely positive; and the effective group count had to support held-group folds. On the admitted support all three hold for the declared endpoint, and they also hold for the pair-specific component that survives a global-response adjustment. The positive control behaves as expected in sign and in scale. Two findings restrict the scope and are part of the decision rather than caveats to it: roughly half of the cycle variance is a global response to the additive prediction, and the panel contains only 499 distinct site pairs, all but eleven of them site pairs the source study itself selected as suspected interactions.

The licensed claim is therefore about measured nonadditivity at source-selected site pairs in resolvable stability states, evaluated against a nonlinear-additive nuisance control, with uncertainty carried at the group level and effect sizes reported in kcal/mol. It is not a claim about the prevalence or magnitude of pairwise interaction across arbitrary position pairs, and it does not license treating the two proteases as independent ground truth or deriving an oracle error ceiling from them.

## The pre-registered acceptance rule does not identify both channels

The protocol's admission rule accepts a row when its numeric `dG_ML` is finite and its combined `deltaG_95CI` lies in [0, 0.5] kcal/mol. That rule constrains the combined stability estimate only. Of the 669,363 rows it accepts, 15,116 carry a trypsin `deltaG_t` exactly at the ±15.000 kcal/mol fit bound and 2,073 carry a chymotrypsin value there; the trypsin channel confidence width on accepted rows reaches 24.3 kcal/mol at its 99th percentile. A single tightly determined combined estimate can thus rest on one identified channel and one unidentified one, and the fit-bound values are boundary artifacts, not point measurements.

The effect on per-channel cycles is large. On the rule exactly as written, the natural-labelled panel has 136,824 cycles and between-channel agreement of Pearson r = +0.165 [+0.131, +0.210], with epsilon standard deviations of 2.855 and 1.876 kcal/mol and a root-mean-square channel difference of 3.162 kcal/mol against a group-equal median absolute difference of only 0.327 kcal/mol. The disagreement is concentrated in a minority of channel-unidentified cycles with very heavy tails.

The admitted support therefore adds one rule, applied identically to both channels: each contributing row must also satisfy 0 ≤ `deltaG_t_95CI` ≤ 0.5 and 0 ≤ `deltaG_c_95CI` ≤ 0.5 kcal/mol. This removes every fit-bound value without a value-dependent filter, and both channels then span −1.573 to +5.961 (trypsin) and −1.094 to +5.514 (chymotrypsin) kcal/mol. The excluded-cohort accounting is explicit: 94,032 of 669,363 accepted rows (14.05%) and 37,314 of 136,824 natural cycles (27.3%) leave the panel, and the distinct site-pair count falls from 513 to 499. The threshold itself is the study's stated lower-confidence boundary already used by the protocol, applied per channel rather than chosen anew.

Every quantity below uses this support, median aggregation by exact (`WT_name`, `aa_seq`) over the same accepted rows for both channels, and the source-derived natural/design labels from the existing disjointness catalogue. Censored `dG_ML` strings (`<-1`: 53,235 rows, `>5`: 43,059 rows, `-`: 4,537 rows) are never coerced to numeric endpoints. Of the 478 catalogue backgrounds, 42 have no accepted WT row and are dropped rather than WT-imputed.

## Between-protease agreement

Two proteases are two assay channels over the same sequences, the same library and the same stability-inference model, not independent measurements of one ground truth. Agreement is reported as a decomposition that respects that. The shared-component standard deviation is the square root of the between-channel covariance; because shared assay error contributes to that covariance, it is an upper bound on the reproducible interaction standard deviation. The per-channel discordance standard deviation is the standard deviation of the channel difference divided by the square root of two; because error common to both channels cancels in the difference, it is a lower bound on per-channel measurement noise. No four-variance oracle ceiling is constructed from these.

All resampling is at the group level, which is what preserves measurement reuse: every cycle of a background shares that background's WT measurement and both of its single-mutant measurements, so resampling whole backgrounds or whole source clusters keeps each shared measurement's signed contribution to its cycles intact, while cycle-level resampling would break exactly that dependence. Intervals below resample source clusters, the coarser unit, with backgrounds weighted equally inside a cluster and cycles equally inside a background. Background-level resampling gives slightly narrower intervals on the same point estimates: r = +0.867 [+0.830, +0.897] overall against +0.885 [+0.847, +0.915] at the cluster level.

## Most of the reproducible cycle variance is a global response

Epsilon on this panel has a large positive mean, +0.533 [+0.425, +0.650] kcal/mol for trypsin and +0.732 [+0.638, +0.835] for chymotrypsin, and it correlates −0.704 (trypsin) and −0.675 (chymotrypsin) with the additive prediction y_A + y_B − y_WT. Subtracting a twenty-quantile-bin response curve in that prediction, fitted per channel over the whole panel, absorbs 53.8% of the trypsin and 51.5% of the chymotrypsin epsilon variance; the fitted response spans 2.53 and 2.44 kcal/mol from its lowest to its highest bin. This is the measurement-side shape of the nonlinear-additive nuisance class the protocol already mandates, and it is the reason that class is mandatory rather than optional.

What survives that adjustment is still reproducible across channels. The adjusted cycle has mean +0.006 and −0.005 kcal/mol, standard deviations 0.581 and 0.587 kcal/mol, r = +0.809 [+0.759, +0.848], shared component SD 0.525 [0.469, 0.582] and per-channel discordance SD 0.255 [0.233, 0.279] kcal/mol, a ratio of 2.06 [1.77, 2.36]. Every stratum retains an interval bounded away from zero, including ≥10 at r = +0.826 [+0.771, +0.865].

Two properties of this adjustment bound its reading. It is fitted in sample on the whole panel, so it removes more than a training-only calibration could, which makes the surviving agreement conservative as a statement about what a held-group nuisance control would leave. Conversely, a twenty-bin step function underfits a steep response, and the additive prediction shares measurement error with epsilon within a channel, so the 52–54% absorbed fraction is an upper bound on the variance a correctly specified global response would explain and the surviving agreement is an upper bound on interaction-specific reproducibility. Both bounds point the same way for the decision: the global response is a large, real part of this endpoint, and it does not exhaust it.

## Positive control on the source study's coupling pair tables

The source study's `pair_name` column names the double-mutant pair tables it scanned for thermodynamic coupling. They resolve to 543 distinct (background, site pair) keys over catalogue backgrounds, and selection is by that source column and its own site numbering alone. This is also the panel's dominant feature: 99,493 of 99,510 natural cycles (99.98%) fall on a documented site pair, across 158 backgrounds and 488 site pairs, and only 17 cycles across 10 backgrounds and 11 site pairs lie outside one. There is no meaningful undocumented comparison set, so the control is a check of conventions and recoverable nonadditivity on the study's own constructs, not a contrast against unselected pairs.

The sign and scale convention is read off the source columns rather than assumed: on 354,993 accepted single-mutant rows, the reported `ddG_ML` matches deltaG(mutant) − deltaG(WT) with a root-mean-square residual of 0.018 kcal/mol, against 2.185 kcal/mol for the opposite sign; the mean reported value is −0.637 kcal/mol, so single substitutions reduce stability on average. Positive deltaG is more stable, so a favourable WT interaction lost by either single substitution must appear as epsilon > 0. That is what the designed pairs show. The hydrogen-bond-network tables, 61,398 cycles on 258 site pairs in 99 backgrounds, give mean epsilon +0.597 (trypsin) and +0.801 (chymotrypsin) kcal/mol with 78.0% and 88.5% of cycles positive and 52.8% and 61.9% above 0.5 kcal/mol in absolute value. The `dmutv5` tables, 38,095 cycles on 230 site pairs in 117 backgrounds, give +0.481 and +0.651 kcal/mol with 69.3% and 80.0% positive. Between-channel agreement is r = +0.908 [+0.875, +0.931] on the hydrogen-bond-network subset and +0.869 [+0.818, +0.904] on `dmutv5`.

The study's own estimand is a different quantity and is not equated with ours. Refitting an additive surface over each of the 473 pair tables that carry at least four cycle-complete double states and taking the residual at those states — our unclipped least-squares stand-in for the study's Bayesian additive fit with its prediction clipped to [−1, 5] kcal/mol, on 115,421 fitted states — gives residuals with standard deviation 0.243 (trypsin) and 0.235 (chymotrypsin) kcal/mol and mean 0.003 kcal/mol, against WT-centered cycles of standard deviation 0.804 and 0.798 kcal/mol on the same 99,464 double states. The two estimands correlate at r = +0.392 and +0.391 (Spearman +0.346 and +0.343) with a root-mean-square difference of 0.925 and 1.045 kcal/mol. A refitted table-level additive baseline absorbs most of what WT-centering leaves in the cycle; the numbers are not interchangeable and no claim below transfers between them.

That residual component reproduces across channels on its own: r = +0.756 [+0.707, +0.799], shared component SD 0.220 [0.196, 0.248], per-channel discordance SD 0.125 [0.119, 0.132] kcal/mol, ratio 1.76 [1.55, 1.99], on 99,464 states across 154 backgrounds, 90 source clusters and 473 site pairs, with every separation stratum bounded away from zero (≥10: r = +0.774 [+0.721, +0.819]). Pair-specific nonadditivity of order 0.2 kcal/mol is therefore resolvable above channel discordance of order 0.13 kcal/mol, which is the tightest evidence here that the instrument carries interaction information and not only a global scale effect. The declared experimental endpoint remains the WT-centered cycle; this residual is a diagnostic, not a substitute target.

## Support geometry and repeated observations

The redundancy of the panel matters more than its cycle count. The 99,510 natural cycles sit on 499 distinct site pairs — a median of 2 site pairs per background (interquartile range 1 to 4, maximum 16), and the median background contributes 186 cycles per site pair, because each table is close to a full amino-acid scan at two positions. Cycles within a site pair share both single-mutant series, so the independent units for an interaction question are the 499 site pairs, 162 backgrounds and 92 source clusters, not the cycles. Sampling frames built from this support shrink accordingly: under the tightened rule, 123 of 162 natural backgrounds carry at least 128 eligible cycles and span 80 source clusters, where the protocol's provisional count on the combined-width rule alone was 138 backgrounds and 86 clusters.

Separation strata are sequence-distance strata and nothing more. Because every retained site pair but eleven was chosen by the study as a suspected interaction, sequence separation here does not separate contacting from non-contacting pairs: the hydrogen-bond-network tables have median separation 8 residues and the `dmutv5` tables 15, and the ≥10 stratum carries a mean epsilon at least as large as the 1–2 stratum (+0.645 [+0.520, +0.776] against +0.486 [+0.300, +0.704] kcal/mol for trypsin). The protocol's requirement that a beyond-local claim appear on non-overlapping-window support is still testable, but a distance contrast on this panel must not be read as a contact contrast.

Repeated rows are codon constructs, not biological replicates. Of 553,064 accepted (`WT_name`, `aa_seq`) states, 17,375 (3.1%) have more than one accepted row, and 17,348 of those have at least two distinct DNA sequences; the largest has 25 rows. Within a repeated state, the absolute deviation from the state median has median 0.085 (trypsin) and 0.056 (chymotrypsin) kcal/mol, 95th percentile 0.358 and 0.241, and maximum 2.246 and 2.727. Replacing the median by the first row in stable source order — the protocol's single-row sensitivity — lowers overall agreement from r = +0.885 [+0.847, +0.915] to +0.830 [+0.786, +0.865], raises epsilon SD from 0.851 to 0.947 kcal/mol and raises per-channel discordance SD from 0.284 to 0.378 kcal/mol. Median aggregation therefore averages some construct-level noise, and the 3.1% repeat coverage is far too sparse and too construct-bound to support a replicate uncertainty model. No inverse-variance weighting or confidence shrinkage is applied anywhere in this qualification.

## Limitations that bind the pairwise experiment

The retained panel is conditioned on resolvability in all four states. Accepted rows have deltaG within [−1, 5] kcal/mol by construction and both channel confidence widths at most 0.5 kcal/mol, so cycles whose double mutant falls below the assay's stability floor are absent. Epsilon estimates here describe resolvable states and are selected against strongly destabilizing doubles.

Site pairs were selected by the source study, so neither the frequency of large nonadditivity on this panel nor its magnitude estimates a population value over protein position pairs. The panel also cannot separate a contact effect from a sequence-distance effect.

Roughly half the endpoint's variance is a global response to the additive prediction. A model increment reported against an additive-only baseline would largely measure that response, so the primary comparison must be against the nonlinear-additive nuisance control, and increments should be reported on the global-response-adjusted scale as well as the raw one.

Between-channel agreement measures reproducibility across two channels that share sequences, library, counts and the proteolysis-model assumptions, including folded-state cleavage and unfolded-state susceptibility prediction. Shared error inflates the shared component and is invisible to the channel difference. Nothing here establishes accuracy against an external thermodynamic measurement, and no oracle ceiling for model prediction is derived from these two channels.

The group universe used for these intervals is the source `WT_cluster` label unioned with nothing else. The admitted family grouping is still being constructed elsewhere and can only coarsen these groups, which would widen the intervals reported here; the 92-cluster and 162-background counts are therefore upper bounds on the final effective group count, and the 1–2 stratum with 36 clusters, 50 site pairs and a Kish effective count of 23.9 is the stratum most exposed to that coarsening.

Absolute epsilon is not channel-invariant: the 0.200 kcal/mol chymotrypsin-minus-trypsin offset means any squared-error statement in kcal²/mol² depends on which channel, or which combination, defines the target. The experiment's declared target is the combined `dG_ML` scale, whose reliability is not directly measured by a between-channel comparison of the two inputs that define it; this qualification bounds that reliability from the channel decomposition rather than certifying the combined estimate.
