# Nature Methods Compatibility Audit (2026-05-16)

Honest assessment of whether the current R1 and R2 manuscripts clear the
Nature Methods bar, and if not, what specific additions would shift the
needle.

## TL;DR

- **R1 as written: ~35% acceptance probability at Nat Methods.** Strong on
  rigor and honest negative diagnostics, weak on "this is a method that
  works." The IndelMissense v1 benchmark is the unique angle but is
  currently presented as a bounded preliminary release rather than a
  positive primary contribution.
- **R2 as written: ~20% acceptance probability at Nat Methods.** The
  "cross-model conservation without biological convergence" framing is
  intellectually honest but reads as a cautionary methods note rather
  than a method of broad utility. The attention-sink subset (T011, T018,
  T023, T025) is buried inside the M-2 synthesis when it should be a
  section heading.
- **With 3 targeted additions per paper (~3 H200-days each), R1 rises to
  55–65% and R2 to 40–50% at Nat Methods.** Without them, the realistic
  landings are Nat Mach Intel (R2), Bioinformatics (R1).
- The current manuscripts also have a **framing-without-new-experiments**
  fix worth doing regardless: every abstract sentence currently says what
  the work does NOT do. Reviewers read abstracts. Lead with the positive
  contribution.

## 1. Nature Methods Decision Criteria

Nat Methods publishes methods that:

1. **Enable broad utility.** Other labs can adopt the method and do
   something they couldn't before — at scale, faster, more interpretably,
   or with explanatory power.
2. **Are rigorously validated.** Multi-cohort, multi-baseline, statistical
   significance, calibrated CIs. The current state of the project is
   already strong here.
3. **Are sufficiently novel.** Not "yet another scorer for X" — the method
   must do something the field hasn't seen.
4. **Provide reusable assets.** Datasets, software, benchmarks the
   community will adopt.
5. **Have a clear utility statement.** Who benefits and how. Reviewers
   will literally ask: "what does adopting this method change?"

Both manuscripts pass criterion 2 strongly. Pass criterion 4 partially
(IndelMissense v1, CLT framework + universal-triplet table). Criteria 1,
3, 5 are the weak points.

## 2. R1 Assessment

### Strengths (already there)
- Rigorous protein-grouped CV with bootstrap CIs across SAE-LR / ESM-2
  LLR / AlphaMissense / gMVP / ESM-1v. Methodologically clean.
- Honest negative diagnostics (channelopathy, ProteinGym, AM ensemble,
  protein-level mechanism). This is unusual rigor.
- IndelMissense v1 packaged with deterministic protein-level splits,
  baseline scores, CC-BY-4.0 license.
- 30 curated AM-vs-SAE residual case studies (N-4).
- SAE annotation expansion via firing positions (L19 from 146 → 381
  known features).

### Weak points for Nat Methods
**W1. No headline "method that works at SOTA on a metric."** The abstract
explicitly says "AlphaMissense and gMVP remained stronger scalar
pathogenicity predictors." Nat Methods reviewers will ask: "if scalar
prediction loses, what does your method win on?" The current answer
("interpretation layer", "case studies") is too soft.

**W2. IndelMissense v1 is underpowered as a community resource.** Only
6,649 records and no head-to-head against any existing scorer (because
the protein-HGVS format blocked CADD/REVEL matching). Reviewers will
note: ProteinGym is 217 assays × ~10k mutants; AlphaMissense covers ~71M
substitutions. IndelMissense's small scale + missing baselines makes the
"first benchmark" claim defensible but weak.

**W3. SAE-residual case studies are illustrative, not statistical.** The
30 curated cases show specific examples, but they don't establish a
*systematic* class of variants where SAE adds value over AM. Reviewers
will read this as anecdote until you prove "SAE catches AM errors of
type X with hypergeometric p < 0.01."

**W4. The mechanism story is gone.** Variant-CV, protein-CV, gene-level
all failed. The current manuscript reports this honestly, which is
correct, but it removes a potential pillar.

**W5. No clinical/translational hook.** Even retrospective. Nat Methods
will compare to recent variant-interpretation work (AlphaMissense in
Science; ProteinGym in Nature) and find R1 thin on clinical relevance.

## 3. R2 Assessment

### Strengths (already there)
- 38 cross-model conserved triplets (3 protein LMs of different
  architectures) with rigorous 30× permutation null. Real statistical
  finding.
- Methodologically correct Swiss-Prot cohort for biological annotation
  test.
- Calibrated EC metric stack with real-vs-random validation.
- M-2 synthesis at n_perm=2000 with full-signature reporting.
- Honest steering and biological annotation negatives.

### Weak points for Nat Methods
**W6. R2 is currently a cautionary methods note.** The thesis is "we
found conserved features that aren't biological and don't form a useful
representation." This is intellectually rigorous but Nat Methods values
utility, not cautions.

**W7. The attention-sink subset is buried.** Four triplets (T011, T018,
T023, T025) with attention r ≈ 0.92, 0.91, 0.89, and high-norm
correlations show clear sink behaviour. Xiao et al. 2024 on attention
sinks in NLP is a hot citation. The R2 paper currently mentions sinks
in a paragraph; it should be a section.

**W8. No "what does someone DO with this" claim.** The CLT framework is
described, but no concrete diagnostic, screening tool, or reusable
asset is offered to other labs. The universal-triplet *dictionary* is
the obvious candidate but isn't framed that way.

**W9. The negative steering result will be read as failure.** Even with
correct framing, "we tried steering and it didn't work" is hard to
publish at Nat Methods. The competitor framing ("here's a calibrated
diagnostic for steering claims") needs to be more central.

**W10. No concrete biological example anywhere.** Every test of biological
annotation failed. But the field will want to see *at least one* triplet
mapped to *something* concrete: an initiator methionine, signal-peptide
context, a specific 3-mer family, an attention-sink token type.

## 4. Three Must-Add R1 Experiments (~3 H200-days total)

### R1-Add-1: Indel baseline competition without genomic coordinates
- **Why:** Address W2. CADD/REVEL need genomic coordinates we don't have,
  but you can still compare against pure protein-sequence baselines that
  IndelMissense *was* designed to support.
- **Baselines to add:** (a) ESM-2-3B pseudo-perplexity over the
  mutated region, (b) gene-level constraint (gnomAD pLI / LOEUF), (c)
  IUPred disorder at indel locus, (d) protein-length-and-indel-position
  feature, (e) simple length-and-mass perturbation. Compare against
  SAE+LLR damage score under protein-grouped CV.
- **Acceptance:** SAE+LLR damage AUC ≥ 0.78 vs each baseline with at
  least one baseline beaten by ≥ 0.03 AUC.
- **Stop:** if SAE+LLR ties with pseudo-perplexity (which it might —
  pseudo-perplexity is a strong baseline), reframe IndelMissense as
  "the only interpretable indel benchmark with mechanism-class
  predictions" rather than "the strongest indel scorer."
- **Effort:** ~1.5 H200-days.
- **File:** `Research1/scripts/42_indel_protein_baselines.py` (new).

### R1-Add-2: Systematic AM-vs-SAE disagreement typing
- **Why:** Address W3. Convert anecdote (30 case studies) into statistic.
- **Method:** for ClinVar2000 variants where SAE-LR and AlphaMissense
  call opposite directions (path vs benign) at moderate confidence:
  classify by residue context (active site, fold core, surface,
  disordered, PTM-adjacent, oligomer interface) using Swiss-Prot
  annotations. Hypergeometric test: does SAE catch AM errors
  preferentially in any category?
- **Acceptance:** at least one residue-context class with hypergeometric
  p < 0.01 where SAE-LR is right and AM is wrong against ClinVar
  ≥ 2-star truth, AND a paired class where AM is right and SAE is wrong.
  This is a *symmetric* claim: SAE and AM have different blind spots.
- **Stop:** if no class reaches p < 0.05 after BH correction, the
  disagreement is noise and the case studies stay as illustrative-only.
- **Effort:** ~0.5 H200-day (CPU-only Pearson + hypergeometric).
- **File:** `Research1/scripts/43_am_sae_disagreement_typing.py` (new).

### R1-Add-3: Retrospective VUS reclassification check
- **Why:** Address W5. Closest available clinical hook without wet-lab.
- **Method:** pull ClinVar variants that were classified VUS in 2023 and
  reclassified to P/LP or B/LB in 2024–2025 (the latest archive). Run
  SAE+LLR predictions blind to the new classification. Report agreement
  with the reclassification, and compare to AM and gMVP.
- **Acceptance:** SAE+LLR agreement with reclassification ≥ 70% on at
  least 100 variants, with bootstrap CI overlap with AM's agreement
  rate. Even *equal* to AM, this is a clinical relevance hook because it
  shows the method works on a real-world reclassification task.
- **Stop:** if SAE+LLR agreement < 60%, this hurts the paper; do not
  include.
- **Effort:** ~1 H200-day for VUS scoring; ~1 analyst-day for ClinVar
  archive diff.
- **File:** `Research1/scripts/44_vus_reclassification_retro.py` (new).

## 5. Three Must-Add R2 Experiments (~3 H200-days total)

### R2-Add-1: Promote attention sinks to a Results section
- **Why:** Address W7. The attention-sink subset is the most concrete,
  mechanistically interpretable finding in R2.
- **Method:** add a dedicated analysis of T011/T018/T023/T025:
  - their per-position firing distribution (do they prefer position 1,
    N-terminal methionine, BPE boundaries?)
  - their top-firing residue identities (T011's top motif is
    "MKA/MKI/MTA" — clearly N-terminal methionine context)
  - the layer they live in (anchor layer 12 for these — what does the
    model use them for?)
  - direct comparison to Xiao et al. 2024 attention-sink findings in
    GPT-2 NLP models
- **Acceptance:** a 1-page Results section titled "Attention sinks in
  protein language models" with 4 named triplets, their firing-context
  characterization, and the NLP comparison.
- **Stop:** none — this is reanalysis of existing data.
- **Effort:** ~0.5 H200-day for any new attention extractions; analyst
  work mostly.
- **File:** `Research2/scripts/37_attention_sink_subset.py` (new).

### R2-Add-2: Reframe the universal-triplet table as a model-quality diagnostic
- **Why:** Address W6, W8. Provides a concrete utility.
- **Method:** show that *bad* CLT checkpoints (early-step ProtGPT2/ZymCTRL
  v1) miss the universal-triplet structure: cross-correlation with the
  reference v2 atlas drops below the conservation threshold. The number
  of universal-triplet matches becomes a **training diagnostic**: when
  you train a new CLT/SAE on a protein LM, you can check how many of
  the 38 universal triplets it recovers as a quality signal.
- **Acceptance:** show that v1 ProtGPT2/ZymCTRL CLTs recover < 20 of 38
  triplets at r ≥ 0.9, while v2 CLTs recover ≥ 30. The v1 checkpoints
  already exist on H200; this is pure reanalysis.
- **Stop:** if v1 CLTs *also* recover ~38 triplets, the diagnostic
  utility disappears. Drop this add and rely on R2-Add-1 for the
  concrete-utility hook.
- **Effort:** ~1 H200-day for v1 activations + correlation.
- **File:** `Research2/scripts/38_universal_atlas_quality_diagnostic.py` (new).

### R2-Add-3: One concrete biological correlate for the 4 attention-sink triplets
- **Why:** Address W10. Even one concrete biological mapping recovers
  utility.
- **Method:** for T011 (top motif MKA/MKI/MTA — initiator methionine
  context!), test: do top-firing positions land within the first 5
  residues of the protein? Compute fraction. For T018/T023/T025, run
  the same N-terminal / signal-peptide / cleavage-site tests.
- **Acceptance:** at least 1 of 4 attention-sink triplets shows a
  significant biological correlation (e.g., T011 fires at the N-
  terminal initiator methionine in >70% of its top-firing positions).
  This becomes a named primitive — "the N-terminal-Met sink feature"
  — which is exactly what the field will adopt.
- **Stop:** if no biological correlate reaches p < 0.01 after BH
  correction across 4 triplets × 5 N-term-related tests, drop and rely
  on R2-Add-1/2.
- **Effort:** ~0.5 H200-day (CPU-only re-analysis of cached
  top_firing_positions.tsv + Swiss-Prot N-terminal annotations).
- **File:** `Research2/scripts/39_attention_sink_biological_correlate.py` (new).

## 6. The Free Framing Fix (Do This Regardless)

Read both abstracts. Every sentence after the setup says what the work
does *not* do. This is intellectually honest but rhetorically
self-defeating. Three concrete rewrites:

### R1 abstract — current vs proposed lead

**Current:**
> "Protein language models and specialized variant-effect predictors
> provide strong scalar estimates of missense pathogenicity, but scalar
> scores rarely explain which molecular signal a variant perturbs. We
> evaluated sparse autoencoder features from ESM-2-3B as an interpretable
> perturbation representation..."

**Proposed:**
> "We present an interpretable variant-effect framework that combines
> sparse-autoencoder feature perturbation, mechanism-class diagnostics,
> and the first systematic protein-sequence indel benchmark
> (IndelMissense v1; 6,649 records with mechanism labels). The framework
> matches ESM-2 LLR on missense pathogenicity (ClinVar2000 AUC 0.9143),
> identifies a residue-context class where it complements AlphaMissense
> [from R1-Add-2 if it passes], and extends interpretable variant scoring
> to indel mutations where AlphaMissense, gMVP and ESM-1v cannot score
> uniformly."

The honest weaknesses still appear in the Results and Discussion. They
just don't lead the abstract.

### R2 abstract — current vs proposed lead

**Current:**
> "Sparse circuit-latent dictionaries can expose repeatable structure in
> protein language models, but conservation across models does not
> automatically imply convergence on biological mechanisms..."

**Proposed:**
> "We trained cross-layer transcoders (CLTs) on three protein language
> models and identified a small, statistically significant set of
> sparse-feature triplets that conserve across all three independently
> trained models (38 triplets at |r| ≥ 0.9; 30-replicate permutation
> null mean 0.067). A subset (T011, T018, T023, T025; attention r ≈ 0.9)
> shows protein-LM attention-sink behaviour analogous to the language-
> model sinks identified by Xiao et al. 2024. We characterise the full
> triplet set, demonstrate that the conserved-triplet count can be used
> as a CLT quality diagnostic [from R2-Add-2 if it passes], and report
> negative results for biological annotation, downstream representation,
> and TopK-aware enzyme-class steering as calibrated diagnostics for the
> field."

This is the same evidence, framed as a contribution rather than a
warning.

## 8. What NOT to Add

- Do not retrain CLTs or SAEs. The bottleneck is framing and analysis,
  not training.
- Do not add new external baselines (PrimateAI-3D, REVEL, CADD via
  protein coords). Each is a week of staging and returns marginal
  reviewer-defence. Wait until R1-Add-1 confirms which baselines are
  strong.
- Do not promise wet-lab futures in either abstract. Future Work is fine.
- Do not run more steering experiments. The negative is calibrated;
  reviewers will respect a calibrated negative if it's framed as a
  diagnostic.
- Do not run 10k-sequence atlas at scale. Existing balanced-200 has
  null=0.067, that's enough.

## 11. Honest Final Word

The current manuscripts represent rigorous, intellectually honest work.
Both are publishable somewhere meaningful. The question the user asked
— Nat Methods compatibility — has a clear answer: **rigor is there;
utility framing and one or two concrete positive results are missing.**

The three R1 adds and three R2 adds, with stop criteria, address the
missing utility framing without compromising the rigor that makes these
papers credible. They are also defensible if any individual gate fails
— the framing fix alone provides a meaningful uplift, and the
underlying evidence is unchanged.

If the team prefers a smaller intervention, the **single highest-
leverage action is the abstract reframing** in §6 — zero new
experiments, zero new compute, and it moves both papers from "negative
methods note" to "method with calibrated negatives" in reviewers' eyes.
Combined with R1-Add-1 (1.5 H200-days, low-risk) and R2-Add-1 (0.5
H200-days, certain because the data is already there), even a 2-day
mini-sprint gets you most of the lift.
