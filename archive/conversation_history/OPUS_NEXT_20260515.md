## 1. Reading M-1 More Carefully

The summary md shows 37/38 categorized, but the per-triplet q-value table
tells a different story than the category counts:

- 25 triplets are k-mer significant (q < 0.05).
- 35 triplets are positional significant.
- 25 triplets are high-norm significant.
- 4 triplets are attention-sink significant.
- 1 triplet is BPE-boundary significant.
- 28 triplets are significant on **3 or more** tests simultaneously.

Looking at concrete rows: T002, T004, T006, T011, T012, T015, T018, T020,
T022, T023, T027, T031, T033, T034, T036 — all show
`kmer q ≈ positional q ≈ high-norm q ≈ 0.0118`. The "0.0118" is the BH-
adjusted permutation floor (n_perm=200, 190 tests). These tests are
hitting the resolution limit and tying.

When all three tests tie, the script's tie-break order
(`k-mer → positional → bpe → attention → high-norm`) deterministically
assigns the triplet to k-mer. T011/T018/T023/T025 each have attention-sink
correlation r ≈ 0.9 and an attention q at the floor; they were assigned
to k-mer purely because k-mer comes first in the order.

So the "21 k-mer / 14 positional / 2 high-norm / 0 attention-sink" headline
underclaims the truth. The honest reading is:

- The 38 conserved triplets are statistically significant on **multiple
  low-level signals at once**.
- Local sequence context (k-mer), normalized position-in-protein, and
  hidden-state magnitude are all entangled, which is biologically
  unsurprising: certain k-mers cluster at certain positions, and high
  hidden norm reflects local context information density.
- A subset (4 triplets, r ≈ 0.9 with received attention) carries a clear
  attention-sink signature — these are likely the "protein-LM analog of
  attention sinks", but the script's tie-break hides them.

This is actually a **better** finding than the original "37 categorized
into 5 disjoint buckets" version. It is the protein-LM equivalent of
"these features fire on tokens with strong local + positional + high-norm
context, sometimes with sink-like attention behaviour." That is the
manuscript's R2 third pillar, correctly stated.

## 2. One Last Synthesis Experiment (no GPU)

### M-2: Unified characterization synthesis
- **Inputs:** existing `triplet_characterization.tsv` and
  `top_firing_positions.tsv`.
- **Tasks:**
  1. **Cross-test overlap matrix.** Build a 5×5 matrix of "triplets
     significant in BOTH test X AND test Y" (q < 0.05). Report Jaccard
     overlap. Expected: high overlap between k-mer, positional, high-norm.
  2. **Re-classify under multi-significance.** For each triplet, list ALL
     significant categories (not just the tie-break winner). Group
     triplets by their full significance signature.
     Expected groups:
     - **kmer-only or kmer-dominant** (likely the "21 k-mer" core)
     - **positional + high-norm** (likely the "14 positional" core)
     - **attention-sink subset** (T011/T018/T023/T025)
     - **multi-everything** (entangled, the largest cluster)
  3. **Per-triplet k-mer motif dump.** For each k-mer-significant triplet,
     report the top-3 enriched 3-mers and 5-mers from `top_k3`/`top_k5`.
     This is already in the TSV — needs surfacing.
  4. **Per-triplet position profile.** For each positional-significant
     triplet, compute the histogram of `position_norm` over its top-100
     firing positions. Classify into N-term-enriched (< 0.2),
     C-term-enriched (> 0.8), centre (0.4–0.6), bimodal, or non-trivial.
- **Output:**
  `Research2/results/circuit_analysis/triplet_synthesis_20260515/`
  - `cross_test_overlap.tsv` — 5×5 Jaccard table
  - `triplet_signatures.tsv` — full significance pattern per triplet
  - `kmer_motifs.tsv` — top motifs per k-mer triplet
  - `positional_profiles.tsv` — position-bucket counts per positional
    triplet
  - `summary.md` — text synthesis suitable for paste into R2 manuscript
- **Acceptance:** synthesis md cleanly states (a) overlap fraction,
  (b) clusters, (c) the attention-sink subset, (d) example motifs.
- **Effort:** ~3 analyst-hours, no GPU.

## 3. Code Review of `35_triplet_characterization.py`

### Issues that affect reported numbers

**A. Permutation count is too low (line 572, `n_perm=200`).** Minimum
permutation p is 1/201 ≈ 0.0050. BH correction across 190 tests collapses
all sub-0.005 p-values into the floor q ≈ 0.0118. The result: many
triplets tie at the same q, and category assignment becomes order-dependent
(see §1). **Fix:** rerun with `n_perm=2000` before the manuscript figure.
~0.5 H200-day. Lower q-floor will separate truly strong from marginal
calls and shift some triplets out of tie-break.

**B. Tie-break is fixed by `category_order` (line 413, 519–521).** Even
after raising n_perm, tied q-values will still occur and silently assign
on the order. **Fix:** for the paper, drop the single-category "assigned"
field and report each triplet's full significance signature. This is
what M-2 does.

**C. Attention is mean-pooled across heads (line 314).** Real attention
sinks (Xiao et al. 2024) are usually concentrated in 1–2 heads. Mean
dilutes the signal. **Fix:** add a parallel `attention_max_over_heads`
metric: `attn[0].max(dim=0).values.mean(dim=0)`. Could add ~5 attention-
sink-significant triplets that the current pipeline misses.

**D. High-norm correlation is partially tautological.** Triplet activation
is `ReLU(W_enc @ resid + b_enc)[feat_idx]`. Hidden norm is `‖resid‖₂`. Both
are derived from the *same* residual stream at the *same* layer, so a
"high activation correlates with high norm" finding is partly a property
of how W_enc projects high-norm vectors. **Fix for paper:** acknowledge
this in the methods section; the high-norm category is not independent of
the activation itself.

### Issues that don't affect numbers but harm maintainability

**E. Module import by file path (line 43–44).** `s33 = load_module(...)`,
then `u29 = s33.u29` chains two file-name imports. Brittle. **Fix:** move
shared helpers (`read_triplets`, `load_model`, `load_trained_clt`,
`token_residue_spans`, `add_token_values_to_residues`) into a proper
package under `Research2/src/analysis/`.

**F. Hard-coded checkpoint paths (line 580–582).** Should live in a YAML
config. Minor.

**G. One-sided positive correlation tests (line 251–252).** Correct for
attention-sink ("we expect sinks to positively correlate"), but means
negative correlations get p=1 silently. For attention, negative correlation
could mean the triplet fires on tokens that *don't* receive attention,
which is also a model-internal signature. **Fix for paper:** add a two-
sided test as a sanity check or explicitly state the one-sided design
choice.

**H. BPE-boundary test (line 211–232).** The v2 fix (one-sided) is
correct. Only T017 passes — no claim depends on this test. Fine as is.

### Recommended action

Only **(A)** affects the published numbers materially. Re-run M-1 with
`n_perm=2000` once before manuscript freeze. ~0.5 H200-day, doesn't change
the qualitative story but lets you report cleaner q-values.

Items (B), (G) are addressed by the M-2 synthesis: report full
significance signatures, not single assignments.

Items (C), (D) become methods-section caveats.

Items (E), (F) are future refactoring, not blockers.

## 5. What Each Manuscript Claims (Final)

### R1 — Nat Methods
Three pillars, all supported, all in current draft after reframe:

1. **Honest competitive baselines.** AM/gMVP/ESM-1v/SAE+LLR on
   ClinVar2000 and Cancer-Holdout-101 under protein-grouped CV. AM is
   strongest scalar (0.9474), gMVP next (0.9369), SAE+LLR competitive
   but not winning (0.9143). Frame: SAE+LLR is competitive, not best.
2. **SAE as interpretation layer.** 30 curated case studies from N-4
   showing variants where AlphaMissense and SAE-LR disagree, with
   feature-firing biological interpretation. Not a competing scorer.
3. **IndelMissense v1.** 6,649 protein-sequence indels with mechanism
   labels, deterministic protein-level splits, baseline AUC 0.7735.
   The first interpretable indel-mechanism benchmark; no peer dataset
   exists.

**Negative diagnostics:** ProteinGym sign-corrected ensemble win rate
33.6%; variant-level mechanism collapses under protein-CV (0.5161);
gene-level mechanism fails under Pfam-family-proxy holdout (0.5665);
channelopathy concordance 62.5%; AM + SAE ensemble does not improve over
AM alone.

### R2 — Nat Methods or Nat Mach Intel
Three pillars:

1. **Cross-model conservation.** 38 universal triplets across 3
   independently trained protein LMs at |r| ≥ 0.9; 30 at |r| ≥ 0.95;
   8 at |r| ≥ 0.98. Permutation null (30 replicates) mean 0.067, max 1.
   Statistical conservation is strong.
2. **Negative interpretation results.** Pfam, Swiss-Prot per-residue,
   DSSP MI all near zero on a Pfam-clan-stratified 500-Swiss-Prot
   cohort. Triplet 38-dim basis fails to match ESM-2 embeddings on
   Pfam-clan classification (1.00 vs 0.40), EC top-class (0.71 vs 0.26),
   and secondary-structure fraction (R² 0.32 vs −0.60). The conserved
   triplets do not encode named biological primitives.
3. **Characterization synthesis.** 37/38 triplets significant on at
   least one of {k-mer, positional, BPE, attention-sink, high-norm}
   under BH-corrected permutation tests. Most are simultaneously k-mer +
   positional + high-norm significant — entangled local-context
   signatures. A subset (4 triplets) shows protein-LM attention-sink
   behaviour. The conserved features capture statistical regularities of
   sequence and model state, not biological structure.

**Negative diagnostics:** EC-class steering 0/8 significant with TopK-
aware on-manifold protocol; selected leads pass Pfam/CLEAN/Foldseek but
generation-wide steering lift is not significant.

**Contribution framing:** "Cross-model conservation in protein language
models: statistical structure without biological convergence."
