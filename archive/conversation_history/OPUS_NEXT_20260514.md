## 1. Why The Triplets Failed Biological Interpretation

The 38 conserved triplets are real (null = 0.067 ± 0.25 over 30 perms).
But:

- They do not separate Pfam families. (N-1 MI ≈ 0)
- They do not separate Swiss-Prot per-residue categories. (N-1 MI ≈ 0)
- They do not separate DSSP secondary structure. (N-1 MI ≈ 0)
- They are not amino-acid detectors. (cheap-label MI ≈ 0.001 nats)
- They are not family detectors. (source-selectivity check earlier: 38/38
  weak/source-mixed for lysozyme vs random)
- They are not a useful linear probe basis. (N-2 fails all 3 tasks)

Whatever they encode is statistically real AND not aligned with any of the
biological coordinates we have tested. The honest scientific question is
now: **what ARE they encoding?** Three live hypotheses are testable from
internal model state alone, without new external data:

- H1: **sub-residue / k-mer context** — they fire on specific 3-mer or
  5-mer patterns invariant to domain context.
- H2: **positional / sequence-architectural** — they fire at specific
  positions (start, end, BPE-boundary, mid-protein region).
- H3: **attention-sink / model-internal** — they fire at positions that
  function as attention sinks across transformer layers, a known artifact
  in large-vocab transformers (Xiao et al. 2024).

Any of these answers is publishable. Architectural-artifact conservation
across protein LMs would be a real methods finding ("interpretability
methods can recover statistical structure that does not map to biology").

## 2. The One Remaining R2 Experiment

### M-1: Characterize the 38 triplets — k-mer / positional / attention
- **Cohort:** reuse the 500 Swiss-Prot sequences from N-1 (already
  cached); add the 200 balanced UniRef50 from earlier runs.
- **Tests (single H200-day, plus 1 analyst-day):**
  1. **k-mer enrichment:** at each triplet's top-100 firing positions,
     compute PWM-style 3-mer and 5-mer preferences centred on the firing
     residue. Test against random-position background. Report MI of
     triplet activation with k-mer identity.
  2. **Positional distribution:** normalized position-in-protein
     (`position / length`). Histogram per triplet. Test against uniform.
  3. **BPE-boundary effect (ProtGPT2 only):** does the triplet fire
     preferentially at sub-residue token boundaries?
  4. **Attention-sink correlation:** for each triplet's top-firing
     positions, compute mean attention received in the same layer (across
     all attention heads). Test correlation with the across-token mean.
  5. **Activation-magnitude baseline:** are triplets simply "high-norm
     hidden state" features? Compute correlation between triplet
     activation and L2-norm of the hidden state at that position.
- **Output:** `Research2/results/circuit_analysis/triplet_characterization_20260514/`
  with `summary.{json,md}` and per-triplet category assignments.
- **Acceptance:** for ≥ 25 of 38 triplets, one of the five tests yields
  statistically significant enrichment (BH-corrected p < 0.05).
  Assignment of each triplet to {k-mer, positional, attention-sink,
  high-norm, unknown} category.
- **Stop criterion:** if < 10 of 38 receive a category, the triplets are
  encoding something we still don't measure; the paper's R2 contribution
  narrows to "statistically conserved latent features that resist
  characterization under standard tests" — still publishable but
  scientifically less satisfying.
- **Effort:** ~1 H200-day for activation/attention extraction; ~1 analyst
  day for analysis.
- **File:** `Research2/scripts/35_triplet_characterization.py` (new).

This is the only R2 experiment worth running. Don't bother with more
annotation strategies or richer cohorts — the triplets have already failed
biological mapping on the right cohort with the right labels.

### R2 — reframe to honest methodology, after M-1
The thesis becomes:

**"Cross-model conservation in protein language models: statistical
structure without biological convergence."**

Three pillars:
1. **Discovery and statistical robustness.** 38 triplets, 30× permutation
   null (mean 0.067, std 0.25), `p << 0.001` for conservation across
   three independently trained protein LMs.
2. **Negative interpretation results, executed correctly.** Pfam /
   Swiss-Prot / DSSP MI all near zero on a Swiss-Prot-anchored 500-
   sequence cohort. Triplet basis fails to match ESM-2 embeddings on
   Pfam, EC, secondary-structure tasks.
3. **Characterization (from M-1).** Categorical assignment to k-mer /
   positional / attention-sink / high-norm / unknown.

This is honest and publishable. Venue: Nat Methods or Nat Mach Intel as a
methods note + cautionary interpretability result. Steering negative
result also goes here as a section.

The paper is *useful*: it tells the protein-LM-interpretability field
which standard interpretation pipelines do and don't work on transcoder
features, with calibrated evidence on both sides. That's the contribution.

## 7. Why Stop Pushing R2 Higher

I've now proposed three brilliant pivots for R2 over four planning
documents: drug-design steering, universal biological primitives, useful
representation basis. Each one has failed cleanly against the evidence.
The team has executed every gate I set, and the data has been definitive
each time. Continuing to propose increasingly creative reframings against
the same negative evidence is wishful thinking, not science.

The 38 conserved triplets are **real**. They are **not biological in any
sense we can currently measure**. That is the actual finding. A paper that
documents this carefully, alongside the calibrated negative steering
result and the corrected methodology, is the honest publishable
contribution from R2 given current state. It is a Nat Methods paper, not
a Nature paper, and pretending otherwise wastes time.

The brilliant work in this project is **R1's IndelMissense v1**. It is
the only unique capability either project has produced that no other
method can replicate today. Frame the R1 paper around it. Freeze. Submit.
