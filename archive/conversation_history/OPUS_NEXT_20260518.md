- **T011/T018/T023 are read-outs of attention sinks, not the sinks

  reconstruction of MLP output. They mirror computation, they don't

## The Diagnostic Reframe of the Causal Failure

Look at the failed ablation result more carefully. T011/T018/T023:

- Fire on positions 1-2 at fraction 1.00 across the top-100 firing rows.
- Correlate with mean attention received at r ≈ 0.92, 0.91, 0.89.
- Enrichment q ≈ 2.7 × 10⁻¹¹⁷ vs background.
- But ablating their CLT features changes nothing (NLL Δ near 0 in 6/9
  conditions; one model has *negative* NLL Δ, the wrong direction).

This is the textbook signature of a **read-out feature**, not a
causal mechanism. In NLP attention-sink literature (Xiao et al. 2024),
attention sinks live in *specific attention heads* that learn to dump
attention onto the start token. The MLP residual stream *reads* the
output of those sinks but does not implement them. Patching the MLP
output should not break the sink.

This insight gives us a sharp follow-up experiment that *should* work
if our interpretation is correct.

## E-1: Direct Attention-Head Sink Ablation (~2 H200-days)

### Why
Test the hypothesis: T011/T018/T023 are CLT readouts of attention-sink
heads. If true, identifying those heads and ablating them directly
should:
1. Produce a measurable NLL change at position 2+ (because sinks
   redistribute attention, and removing them changes downstream
   prediction).
2. Reduce T011/T018/T023 activation at positions 1-2 toward background.

The same intervention also gives us a positive method: **"sparse-
feature-guided attention-head identification."**

### Method
For each of ProtGPT2 v2, ZymCTRL v2, ProGen2-medium:

1. **Identify attention-sink heads** following Xiao et al. 2024:
   compute per-head attention-received concentration at position 1
   across 100 Swiss-Prot proteins. Heads with > 50% attention received
   at position 1 are candidate sinks.
2. **Pair sink heads with the T011/T018/T023 triplet for that model.**
   For each candidate sink head, regress its position-1 attention
   against the corresponding triplet feature activation at the same
   sequences. Sink heads paired with the triplet should show r ≥ 0.5.
3. **Ablate paired sink heads directly** by zeroing the head's
   output at positions 1-2 (standard head-ablation hook on
   `block.attn`). Measure:
   - Teacher-forced ΔNLL at positions 2-10 and 11+
   - T011/T018/T023 activation at positions 1-2 (post-ablation)
   - Attention redistribution cosine distance
4. **Compare against three controls:**
   - Random non-sink head in the same layer
   - T025 (the non-N-terminal triplet) paired head if any
   - Same layer, different unrelated feature

### Gates
- **PASS (Nat Methods-grade):** ≥ 2 of 3 models show that ablating the
  triplet-paired sink head raises NLL at positions 2-10 by ≥ 0.5 nats
  with p < 0.05, while random-head controls show no effect. AND triplet
  feature activation drops at least 50 % at positions 1-2 post-ablation.
- **PARTIAL (Nat Mach Intel-grade):** effect appears in one model or
  only in feature-activation-drop, not NLL.
- **FAIL:** no effect beyond controls. We submit R2 with the current
  correlation-and-quality-diagnostic framing.

### Honest expected outcome
P(PASS) ≈ 35–45 %. The hypothesis is the most parsimonious explanation
of the failed CLT ablation, but it is not guaranteed. Decoder-only
protein LMs may use attention sinks differently from NLP transformers,
or the sinks may be distributed across many heads.

### Cost
~2 H200-days. The 1-GPU pod must be re-reserved.

### File
`Research2/scripts/41_attention_head_sink_ablation.py` (new).

## E-2 (Conditional): Sparse-Feature-Guided Head Discovery as a Method

If E-1 PASSES, we have the headline that R2 has been searching for:

> Cross-model conserved sparse-feature triplets identify attention-sink
> heads in protein language models. Direct ablation of these heads
> produces predictable NLL and attention-redistribution effects across
> three independently trained models, validating the triplets as a
> **diagnostic tool for locating attention-sink heads from sparse
> features alone**.

This is a *method*, not just a discovery. The community can apply this
to any new protein LM: train a CLT, find triplet-like features, use
them to localize the sink heads, then prune / quantize / steer them.
That's Nat Methods utility framing.

If E-1 PASSES, also run:

### E-2: Cross-Architecture Generalization
Re-run the universal triplet matching pipeline on a fourth, *encoder-
based* protein LM: ESM-2-3B. We already have SAE checkpoints from R1.
Do the same SAE-feature triplets appear in ESM-2-3B at the
corresponding layer? If yes, this extends the convergence claim from
"three decoder-only protein LMs" to "decoder + encoder protein LMs"
— a much stronger reviewer-defence claim.

Cost: ~1 H200-day.
